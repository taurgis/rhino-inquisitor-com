### C.4 Pseudorandomness in LLMs

Most machine learning models, including LLMs, use pseudorandom number generators in one form or another to produce their outputs. This is because the pseudorandom AI-generated distribution is a collection of Dirac delta function distributions, and a human is exorbitantly unlikely to produce a sample corresponding to any of the delta functions. In our framework, this means that the TV between the human and pseudorandom AI-generated distributions is almost one, making the bound in Theorem [1] vacuous.

We argue that although the true TV between the human and pseudorandom AI-generated distributions is high and there exists (in theory) a detector function that can separate the distributions almost perfectly, this function may not be efficiently computable.

We modify the bound in Theorem [1] to include a negligible correction term ϵ\\epsilon to account for the use of pseudorandomness. We prove that the performance of a polynomial-time computable detector DD on a pseudorandom version of the AI-generated distribution ℳ^\\widehat{\\mathcal{M}} is bounded by the total variation for the truly random distribution ℳ\\mathcal{M} (resulting from the LLM using true randomness) as follows:

|  | 𝖠𝖴𝖱𝖮𝖢⁡(D)≤12+𝖳𝖵⁡(ℳ,ℋ)−𝖳𝖵​(ℳ,ℋ)22+ϵ.\mathsf{AUROC}(D)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\mathsf{TV}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H})-\frac{\mathsf{TV}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H})^{2}}{2}+\epsilon. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

The term ϵ\\epsilon represents the gap between the probabilities assigned by ℳ\\mathcal{M} and ℳ^\\widehat{\\mathcal{M}} to any polynomial-time computable {0,1}\\{0,1\\}\-function ff, i.e.,

|  | \|Ps∈ℳ[f(s)=1]−Ps∈ℳ^[f(s)=1]\|≤ϵ.\big\|\mathbb{P}_{s\in\mathcal{M}}[f(s)=1]-\mathbb{P}_{s\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}[f(s)=1]\big\|\leq\epsilon. |  | (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

This term is orders of magnitude smaller than any of the terms in the bound and can be safely ignored. From a computational point of view, the TV for the pseudorandom distribution is almost the same as the truly random AI-generated distribution.

Computational Total Variation Distance. Just as the total variation distance 𝖳𝖵\\mathsf{TV} between two probability distributions is defined as the difference in probabilities assigned by the two distributions to any {0,1}\\{0,1\\}\-function, we define a computational version of this distance 𝖳𝖵c\\mathsf{TV}\_{c} for polynomial-time computable functions:

|  | 𝖳𝖵c(A,B)=maxf∈𝒫\|Ps∼A[f(s)=1]−Ps∼B[f(s)=1]\|,\mathsf{TV}_{c}(A,B)=\max_{f\in\mathcal{P}}\big\|\mathbb{P}_{s\sim A}[f(s)=1]-\mathbb{P}_{s\sim B}[f(s)=1]\big\|, |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where 𝒫\\mathcal{P} represents the set of polynomial-time computable {0,1}\\{0,1\\}\-functions. 𝒫\\mathcal{P} could also be defined as the set of all polynomial-size circuits which could be more appropriate for deep neural network-based detectors.

###### Lemma 1.

The area under the ROC of any polynomial-time computable detector DD is bounded as

|  | 𝖠𝖴𝖱𝖮𝖢⁡(D)≤12+𝖳𝖵c​(ℳ^,ℋ)−𝖳𝖵c​(ℳ^,ℋ)22.\mathsf{AUROC}(D)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\mathsf{TV}_{c}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H})-\frac{\mathsf{TV}_{c}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H})^{2}}{2}. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

This lemma can be proved in the same way as Theorem [1] by replacing the truly random AI-generated distribution ℳ\\mathcal{M} with its pseudorandom version ℳ^\\widehat{\\mathcal{M}} and the true total variation 𝖳𝖵\\mathsf{TV} with its computaional variant 𝖳𝖵c\\mathsf{TV}\_{c}.

Next, we relate the computational total variation 𝖳𝖵c\\mathsf{TV}\_{c} between ℋ\\mathcal{H} and the pseudorandom distribution ℳ^\\widehat{\\mathcal{M}} with the total variation 𝖳𝖵\\mathsf{TV} between ℋ\\mathcal{H} and the truly random distribution ℳ\\mathcal{M}.

### C.4 Pseudorandomness in LLMs

Most machine learning models, including LLMs, use pseudorandom number generators in one form or another to produce their outputs. For example, an LLM may use a pseudorandom number generator to sample the next token in the output sequence. In discussing our hardness result, [Kirchenbauer et al. (2023b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib26) in a more recent work argue that this pseudorandomness makes the AI-generated text distribution very different from the human-generated text distribution. This is because the pseudorandom AI-generated distribution is a collection of Dirac delta function distributions, and a human is exorbitantly unlikely to produce a sample corresponding to any of the delta functions. In our framework, this means that the TV between the human and pseudorandom AI-generated distributions is almost one, making the bound in Theorem [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 4 Hardness of Reliable AI Text Detection ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") vacuous.

We argue that although the true TV between the human and pseudorandom AI-generated distributions is high and there exists (in theory) a detector function that can separate the distributions almost perfectly, this function may not be efficiently computable. Any polynomial-time computable detector can only achieve a negligible advantage from the use of pseudorandomness instead of true randomness. If we had knowledge of the seed used for the pseudorandom number generator, we would be able to predict the pseudorandom samples. However, an individual seeking to evade detection could simply randomize this seed making it computationally infeasible to predict the samples.

We modify the bound in Theorem [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 4 Hardness of Reliable AI Text Detection ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") to include a negligible correction term ϵ\\epsilon to account for the use of pseudorandomness. From a computational point of view, the TV for the pseudorandom distribution is almost the same as the truly random AI-generated distribution. Thus, our framework provides a reasonable approximation for real-world LLMs, and the hardness result holds even in the presence of pseudorandomness.

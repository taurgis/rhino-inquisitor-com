## Detailed

### 3.4 Metrics

#### Windowed prevalence.

We quantify lexical usage via _windowed prevalence_, as this reduces sensitivity to local repetition and improves cross-lingual comparability. For each continuation dd, we consider a fixed-size window of K\=12K{=}12 tokens. With 100,000 prompts per language, this gives about 1.2M model tokens and 1.2M human tokens per language for estimating AI overuse. For word ww, we define the indicator

|  | Id(w)=𝟏[w∈Windowd].I_{d}(w)=\mathbf{1}\bigl[w\in\text{Window}_{d}\bigr]. |  | (1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

The corpus-level prevalence count is then

|  | c⁡(w)=∑d=1NId​(w),c(w)=\sum_{d=1}^{N}I_{d}(w), |  | (2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

|  | ℓ⁡(w)=c⁡(w)+12N+1.\ell(w)=\frac{c(w)+\tfrac{1}{2}}{N+1}. |  | (3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

#### Log Prevalence Ratio.

We compute ℓH​(w)\\ell\_{H}(w) and ℓM​(w)\\ell\_{M}(w) for human and for model continuations. The _Log Prevalence Ratio_ (lpr) is defined as

|  | lpr​(w)=log⁡ℓM​(w)ℓH​(w).\text{{lpr}{}}(w)=\log\frac{\ell_{M}(w)}{\ell_{H}(w)}. |  | (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Positive values indicate AI overuse. To reduce instability for rare items, we require a _count guard_ of cM​(w)≥20c\_{M}(w){\\geq}20 before computing lpr; items below this threshold are assigned lpr\=0\\textsc{lpr}{}{=}0. Ranking words by lpr gives per-language lists of AI-overused items. Our primary analysis focuses on _content words_ (NOUN, VERB, ADJ, ADV), to align our results with the literature on spiking lexical items. We also report all-word results in Appendix [F.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25358v1#A6.SS2 "F.2 Selected All-Word Lists ‣ Appendix F Selected AI-Overuse Lists ‣ AI-Associated Lexical Shifts Across 34 Languages:Cross-Lingual Convergence and Diachronic Uptake in News Writing").

Jeffreys smoothing allows us to retain items with cH​(w)\=0c\_{H}(w)=0 whilst avoiding undefined or infinite increases when moving from cH​(w)\=0c\_{H}(w)=0 to cM​(w)\>0c\_{M}(w)>0. This is particularly important because many of the strongest overrepresented items are extremely rare in the matched human text. (Note that the analyses in §[3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25358v1#S3.SS5 "3.5 Pre- vs Post-GPT Analysis ‣ 3 Data and Methods ‣ AI-Associated Lexical Shifts Across 34 Languages:Cross-Lingual Convergence and Diachronic Uptake in News Writing") and §[3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25358v1#S3.SS6 "3.6 Diachronic Analysis ‣ 3 Data and Methods ‣ AI-Associated Lexical Shifts Across 34 Languages:Cross-Lingual Convergence and Diachronic Uptake in News Writing") operate on raw WMT corpus token counts, not on smoothed values.)

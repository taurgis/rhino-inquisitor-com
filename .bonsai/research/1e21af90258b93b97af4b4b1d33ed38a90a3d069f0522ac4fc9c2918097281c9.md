### 5.1 Hard Negative Mining

Hard negative mining has long been used as a technique for object detection in computer vision, as the problem of detecting semantically meaningful objects in images is severely class imbalanced (images are mostly background and therefore most patches are too easy to classify). ([Felzenszwalb et al., 2010](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib30)) ([Shrivastava et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib31)). Our work is related to this problem although we are operating in the language domain rather than the image domain, because most documents are quite easy to classify. Human documents, such as reviews, are often all very similar to each other, and AI documents often have dead giveaways (such as overusing phrases like "delve" and "it is important to note" or "as an AI language model") and so once the model learns from a few examples that a _category_ of text is likely to be human or AI, it does not need to see more examples from the category.

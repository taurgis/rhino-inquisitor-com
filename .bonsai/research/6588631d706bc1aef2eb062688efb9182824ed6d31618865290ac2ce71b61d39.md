## 4 Does writing with LLMs result in more similar essays?

In the previous section, we have seen that models contribute a substantial portion of the essays’ content. Does this lead to more similar essays, or do the essays retain the individual styles of the users? In this section, we measure the homogenization effect of co-writing with LLMs.

Figure 2: Boxplots of homogenization scores for all three groups (Solo, InstructGPT, GPT3) when comparing essays at the key point level using Rouge-L as the similarity metric. The left-most column (Total) shows essay homogenization scores for all topics and the other columns show the scores for essays on each topic. Essays written with InstructGPT exhibit higher corpus homogenization by a statistically significant margin ([Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S4.SS2 "4.2 Results ‣ 4 Does writing with LLMs result in more similar essays? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")) and the highest median topic homogenization on 77 out of 1010 topics. We observe the same trend when using BertScore in [Figure 9(b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3.F9.sf2 "In Figure 9 ‣ Writing with InstructGPT results in more similar content at the key point and raw essay level ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?") in [Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3 "Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?").

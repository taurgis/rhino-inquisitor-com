## Detailed

### 6.3 Can LLMs Self Identify “Slop”?

Recent text evaluations have prompted LLMs to judge text qualities not readily captured by existing metrics (e.g., [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#bib.bib41); [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#bib.bib18)). This is usually done zero-shot, providing instructions for evaluation. Here we test three LLMs (GPT-5, Deepseek-V3, and o3-mini) for their ability to (a) predict binary “slop” labels and (b) extracting “slop” spans. In both cases, we provide the full “slop” guide given to annotators (See Appendix [L](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#A12 "Appendix L LLM Evaluations ‣ Measuring AI “Slop” in Text") for the full prompt given to each model).

Results: Predicting Binary Slop Labels Table [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#S6.T7 "Table 7 ‣ 6.3 Can LLMs Self Identify “Slop”? ‣ 6 Automatically Measuring Slop ‣ Measuring AI “Slop” in Text") shows the results of binary slop prediction for GPT-5, Deepseek-V3, and o3-mini. Agreement with human annotators is low; κ\\kappa values are ∼\\sim0\. Models under-predict the slop label (0.03-0.08), especially relative to humans (0.35). Both recall (0.08-0.12) and precision (0.25-0.42) are (very) low across all models, showing LLMs do a poor job at this task.

#### 6.3.1 LLM-Generated Span Rationales

In addition to extracting spans, the LLM-as-a-judge is instructed to provide a rationale for each selection. Reasoning chains do not reliably return the exact codes from the guide, so to assess the overall alignment of the reasoning and the human-assigned codes, we count and rank tri-grams from the judge-generated rationale (Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#S6.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 6.3.1 LLM-Generated Span Rationales ‣ 6.3 Can LLMs Self Identify “Slop”? ‣ 6 Automatically Measuring Slop ‣ Measuring AI “Slop” in Text"), Appendix Figure [8](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163#A7.F8 "Figure 8 ‣ Appendix G Human-LLM Span Overlap (Qualitative Assessment ‣ Measuring AI “Slop” in Text")).

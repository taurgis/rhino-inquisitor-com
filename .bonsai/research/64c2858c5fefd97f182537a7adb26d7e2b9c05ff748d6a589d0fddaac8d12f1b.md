### 3.6 Adversarial Attacks

When selecting adversarial attacks, we assume that our adversary has exactly one query and no knowledge of the detector. Thus, we include the following 11 black-box, query-free attacks as opposed to gradient-based methods:

1.  1.
    
    Alternative Spelling: Use British spelling
    
2.  2.
    
    Article Deletion: Delete (‘the’, ‘a’, ‘an’)
    
3.  3.
    
    Add Paragraph: Put \\n\\n between sentences
    
4.  4.
    
    Upper-Lower: Swap the case of words
    
5.  5.
    
    Zero-Width Space: Insert the zero-width space U+200B every other character
    
6.  6.
    
    Whitespace: Add spaces between characters
    
7.  7.
    
    Homoglyph: Swap characters for alternatives that look similar, e.g. e →\\rightarrow e (U+0435)
    
8.  8.
    
    Number: Randomly shuffle digits of numbers
    
9.  9.
    
    Misspelling: Insert common misspellings
    
10.  10.
11.  11.
     
     Synonym: Swap tokens with highly similar BERT [Devlin et al. (2019)] candidate tokens
     

Following recommendations from [Dyrmishi et al. (2023)], we manually reviewed our data to ensure that adversarial attacks were inconspicuous. Thus, for each attack only a small percentage of the total available mutations were actually carried out.

Details on the mutation percentages for each attack as well as specifics about implementation can be found in Appendix [A.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#A1.SS4 "A.4 Adversarial Attacks ‣ Appendix A Dataset Details ‣ RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors").

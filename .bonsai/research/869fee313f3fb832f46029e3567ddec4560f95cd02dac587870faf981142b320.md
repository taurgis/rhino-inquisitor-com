## 7 Related Work

#### Human-AI collaborative writing.

Collaborative writing predates LLMs where users are assisted by suggestions retrieved from a knowledge base ([Swanson and Gordon, 2008]; [Swanson and Gordon, 2012]; [Chen et al., 2014]; [Roemmele and Gordon, 2015]) or generated by task-specific RNNs ([Ghazvininejad et al., 2017]; [Roemmele and Gordon, 2018]; [Clark et al., 2018]). In contrast, LLMs offer suggestions that are often directly incorporated into user writing, either through continuations or in response to instructions, resulting in a surge of studies demonstrating their effectiveness ([Gero and Chilton, 2019]; [Akoury et al., 2020]; [Yuan et al., 2022]; [Swanson et al., 2021]; [Chakrabarty et al., 2022]; [Ippolito et al., 2022]; [Mirowski et al., 2023]) However, despite the improved productivity, we show that LLM-contributed text can also affect the writing in subtle and unintended ways.

#### Effect of human-AI co-writing.

Recent research focuses on analyzing user-model interactions and their influence on users and the writing process. [Lee et al. (2022a)] collect data on user interactions with GPT3 as a collaborator, noting increased vocabulary diversity and productivity. Our work extends this research by formalizing metrics to quantify the homogenization effects resulting from user interactions with LLMs by comparing collaborative essays with essays written without model help.

#### Evaluation of interactive text generation.

Traditional evaluation metrics primarily assess text similarity to reference text ([Papineni et al., 2002]; [Lin, 2004]; [Sellam et al., 2020]). Their experiments show a trade-off between the quality and diversity of output generated by both image and language models, investigating different model sizes and decoding strategies. Our work builds on the recommendations in [Pillutla et al. (2023)] to propose metrics for diversity that can be used across model families and scales, further showing the relationship between diversity and aligning LLMs with human feedback. These include user-rated model helpfulness ([Roemmele and Gordon, 2018]; [Clark et al., 2018]; [Padmakumar and He, 2022]), retention of model suggestions in the final text ([Akoury et al., 2020]), and completion time ([Buschek et al., 2021]). Our work extends existing metrics that evaluate lexical diversity and text style based on token and POS n-grams statistics ([Roemmele et al., 2017]; [See et al., 2019]; [Tevet and Berant, 2021]; [Meister et al., 2023]). We adapt these metrics for co-writing scenarios and introduce content-specific diversity measures.

#### Social impacts of LLMs.

[Bommasani et al. (2021)]; [Bender et al. (2021)]; [Solaiman et al. (2023)] extensively discuss the societal risks of the adoption of LLMs in user-facing applications. [Jakesch et al. (2023)]; [Bhat et al. (2023)] show that writing with a biased language model can change user opinions. Our work investigates one axis of such evaluation, namely content diversity, in co-writing settings.

## 7 Related Work

#### Human-AI collaborative writing.

Collaborative writing predates LLMs where users are assisted by suggestions retrieved from a knowledge base ([Swanson and Gordon, 2008](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib49); [Swanson and Gordon, 2012](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib50); [Chen et al., 2014](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib11); [Roemmele and Gordon, 2015](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib39)) or generated by task-specific RNNs ([Ghazvininejad et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib17); [Roemmele and Gordon, 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib40); [Clark et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib12)). The early systems can produce relevant suggestions, but they often need to be revised before being incorporated into the draft. In contrast, LLMs offer suggestions that are often directly incorporated into user writing, either through continuations or in response to instructions, resulting in a surge of studies demonstrating their effectiveness ([Gero and Chilton, 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib16); [Akoury et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib1); [Yuan et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib53); [Swanson et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib48); [Chakrabarty et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib10); [Ippolito et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib20); [Mirowski et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib31)) However, despite the improved productivity, we show that LLM-contributed text can also affect the writing in subtle and unintended ways.

#### Effect of human-AI co-writing.

Recent research focuses on analyzing user-model interactions and their influence on users and the writing process. [Buschek et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib8) investigate how the number of suggestions affects users in email writing, highlighting the tradeoff between suggestion utility and writing efficiency. [Lee et al. (2022a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib23) collect data on user interactions with GPT3 as a collaborator, noting increased vocabulary diversity and productivity. Our work extends this research by formalizing metrics to quantify the homogenization effects resulting from user interactions with LLMs by comparing collaborative essays with essays written without model help.

#### Evaluation of interactive text generation.

Traditional evaluation metrics primarily assess text similarity to reference text ([Papineni et al., 2002](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib35); [Lin, 2004](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib28); [Sellam et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib44)). [Pillutla et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib38); [Liu et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib29); [Pillutla et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib37) propose _Mauve_, a metric that measures the match between the distribution of output produced by a generative model and a reference distribution. Their experiments show a trade-off between the quality and diversity of output generated by both image and language models, investigating different model sizes and decoding strategies. Our work builds on the recommendations in [Pillutla et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib37) to propose metrics for diversity that can be used across model families and scales, further showing the relationship between diversity and aligning LLMs with human feedback. In interactive contexts where references are absent, alternative metrics gauge model assistance effectiveness indirectly. These include user-rated model helpfulness ([Roemmele and Gordon, 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib40); [Clark et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib12); [Padmakumar and He, 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib34)), retention of model suggestions in the final text ([Akoury et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib1)), and completion time ([Buschek et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib8)). [Lee et al. (2022b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib24) additionally considers user ownership and enjoyment which emphasizes maintaining user writing style. Our work extends existing metrics that evaluate lexical diversity and text style based on token and POS n-grams statistics ([Roemmele et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib41); [See et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib43); [Tevet and Berant, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib52); [Meister et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib30)). We adapt these metrics for co-writing scenarios and introduce content-specific diversity measures.

#### Social impacts of LLMs.

[Bommasani et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib7); [Bender et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib4); [Solaiman et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib45) extensively discuss the societal risks of the adoption of LLMs in user-facing applications. Most relevant to our work is the concern over the potential emergence of an algorithmic monoculture ([Kleinberg and Raghavan, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib22)), which assesses outcome homogenization in decision-making [Creel and Hellman (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib13); [Bommasani et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib6). Additionally, [Hancock et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib19) discuss the potential language homogenization during AI-mediated communication, while [Arnold et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib2) demonstrate that predictive keyboards lead to shorter image captions with fewer rare words. [Jakesch et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib21); [Bhat et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib5) show that writing with a biased language model can change user opinions. [Liao and Xiao (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib27) advocate for novel evaluation methods to uncover socio-technical issues arising from increased general-purpose LLM use. Our work investigates one axis of such evaluation, namely content diversity, in co-writing settings.

## Detailed

### 5.4 Deep Learning Based Classifiers

Several deep learning based classifiers have also emerged as alternatives to probability based classifiers. OpenAI’s classifier ([OpenAI, 2023b](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib37)) is a RoBERTa based deep learning model finetuned to predict whether a document is AI-written or not based on a large training set composed of both open-source pretraining data and AI-written data generated by OpenAI’s API. Due to its low rate of accuracy, it was discontinued in 2023. More recently, SeqXGPT ([Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib38)) is an example of a hybrid classifier that uses both probabilistic and deep learning elements to show strong performance on sentence level AI classification. It uses extracted perplexities from a variety of language models, and then runs them through a relatively shallow neural network to predict whether sentences are ChatGPT-generated or not. Ghostbuster ([Verma et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib18)) is another recent work that trains a shallow classifier to detect AI-generated text based on combinations of unigram, trigram, and GPT-3.5 embedding features. A recent study ([Pu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib39)) has shown that deep learning based AI-generated text classifiers exhibit some generalization ability to detect text from larger models when trained on outputs of smaller models from the same family. Finally, RADAR ([Hu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3#bib.bib40)) uses an adversarial learning approach to increase the robustness of a deep learning based AI detector to paraphrasing attacks. While showing significant progress in detection robustness, like DetectGPT, it is a white-box method, meaning a different detector must be used for each individual LLM, limiting its effectiveness in practice.

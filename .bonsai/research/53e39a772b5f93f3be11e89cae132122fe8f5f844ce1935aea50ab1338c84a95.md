### 2.2 Computational Approaches to Rhetoric and Style

Computational stylistics establishes that individual writing style is a measurable fingerprint detectable through quantitative analysis [Neal et al. (2017)]. Stylometry leverages metrics such as Burrows’ Delta to measure the distribution of most frequent words, providing a content-independent authorship signature [Burrows (2002)]; [Jannidis et al. (2015)]. Computational rhetorical analysis has sought to map discourse structure through frameworks such as rhetorical structure theory and the automated detection of complex figures and tropes [Majdik and Graham (2024)]; [Erhardt (2025)]. These stylometric tools have more recently been applied to AI-generated text detection, identifying distinctive patterns such as reduced lexical diversity and increased structural uniformity relative to human expert writing [Aityan et al. (2026)]; [Al-Shaibani and Ahmed (2026)].

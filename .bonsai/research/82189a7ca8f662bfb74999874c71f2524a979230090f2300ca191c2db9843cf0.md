## Update: 7 October 2023

Very good progress in Safari 17. Tables and description lists are no longer broken when `display` properties are applied. Buttons with `display: contents`, however, are still inoperable by keyboard users and problematic for VO users (and I confirmed is also the case in Safari TP 180).

Meanwhile, the heading issue I reported for Safari 17 on iPadOS ([261978 – AX: Headings with \`display: contents\` cannot be navigated](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261978)) has been marked as a VoiceOver issue with no insight when it will be fixed. But marking it a VoiceOver issue means Safari can claim to have no bug so yay?

Apple seems reasonably confident it has finally fixed its historically years-lagging support (despite prior claims), and so has been doing the rounds suddenly arguing all the other browsers and specs need to fix `display: contents` issues while using its own claims of (abruptly and questionably) better support to bolster them:

*   CSS Working Group [Comment on #3040](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/3040#issuecomment-1750967636) \[css-a11y\]\[css-display\] display: contents; strips semantic role from elements from 2018.
*   Web Platform Tests [#568 `display: contents`](https://github.com/web-platform-tests/interop/issues/568)

I also filed a [PR with Can I Use](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse/pull/6853) to amend the one [filed by Apple three weeks ago](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse/pull/6833) (I was unable to review owing to travel and this is not my job slash no one is paying me).

With Safari almost there on basic support and Apple now pushing for the specs and browsers to agree, after sitting it out for a few years, I am excited that the end is in sight. Which I expect before WCAG 3.

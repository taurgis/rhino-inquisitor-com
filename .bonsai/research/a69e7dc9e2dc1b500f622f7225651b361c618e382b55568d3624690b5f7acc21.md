### 3.2 Results and Discussion

Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14729v1#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.2 Results and Discussion ‣ 3 Experiment 1 ‣ The misclassification of autistic writing as AI-generated") shows the regression coefficients and Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14729v1#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.2 Results and Discussion ‣ 3 Experiment 1 ‣ The misclassification of autistic writing as AI-generated") shows the effect plots for significant variables of this model. We see that the subcorpus a post came from was a significant predictor in determining whether or not it was flagged as AI-generated. Based on the odds-ratio, posts from the likely-autistic subcorpus had a 25% greater chance of being classified as AI-generated. The model also showed a significant negative effect of perplexity, which matches expectations. Posts with higher perplexity were less likely to be flagged as AI. Lastly, we see that the length of each post was significant in determining whether or not a post would be flagged as AI-generated. Shorter posts tended to be flagged as AI more often than longer posts. This tracks with the conventional wisdom that AI-detectors are less accurate with shorter texts. If the model tends to more often flag shorter texts as AI-generated, even above the 1000-character minimum threshold suggested, this is something worth exploring in greater depth.

### 3.5 Decoding Strategies

The decoding strategy determines how tokens are selected from the language model’s probability distribution. Previous work has shown that greedy decoding reduces the diversity of text and makes it easier to detect while sampling directly from the distribution shows the opposite effect ([Ippolito et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib34)). Based on these findings, we generate two outputs per prompt, one with greedy decoding and the other with fully random sampling.

We also generate two additional outputs with [Keskar et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib36)’s repetition penalty when available.22 2 Only open-source models provide a repetition penalty. Closed-source model APIs instead offer slightly different “frequency” and “presence” penalties (see Appendix [A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#A1.SS5 "A.5 Repetition Penalty vs. Frequency Penalty vs. Presence Penalty ‣ Appendix A Dataset Details ‣ RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors")). This penalty works by down-weighting the probability of tokens that have previously appeared in the context window by some multiplicative factor θ\\theta, resulting in less repetitive output. We are the first to evaluate this penalty for detection and show that it significantly reduces the detectability of outputs. Following [Keskar et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib36), we use θ\=1.2\\theta=1.2 for our experiments.

## 6 Results

In this section, we examine how effectively participants post-edited LLM-generated text to sound like themselves ([§ 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS1 "6.1 Effectiveness of Post-Editing in Improving Stylistic Authenticity ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")) and the style of post-edited text itself ([§ 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS2 "6.2 Post-Editing and Stylistic Homogeneity ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")). We also discuss participants’ perception of style ([§ 6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS3 "6.3 Do Human Perceptions of Stylistic Similarity Agree with Model Measures? ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")), the features participants do and do not post-edit ([§ 6.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS4 "6.4 What Are(n’t) Participants Editing? ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")), and participants’ reasons to use or not use LLM drafts in future writing where personal style is important ([§ 6.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS5 "6.5 (Why) Do Participants Like to Post-Edit? ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")).

Unless otherwise stated, group comparisons were performed using permutation tests (see [§ A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A1.SS2 "A.2 Permutation Test Details ‣ Appendix A Additional Experimental Details ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")), with corresponding effect sizes reported as Hedges’ gg [Hedges (1981)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#bib.bib26) with 95%95\\% confidence intervals calculated from 10001000 bootstrap samples. Associations were analyzed using repeated-measures correlation [Bakdash and Marusich (2017)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#bib.bib22). Claims of statistical significance for preregistered analyses refer to results surviving Benjamini–Hochberg correction [Benjamini and Hochberg (1995)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#bib.bib25), controlling the false discovery rate at q\=0.05q=0.05. Raw pp\-values are reported throughout.1111 11 Exploratory analyses were not included in this multiple-testing correction and are identified as such.

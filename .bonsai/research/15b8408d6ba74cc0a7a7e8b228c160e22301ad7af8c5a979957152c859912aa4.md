## Detailed

### 2.1 Language choice

When selecting languages and corpora for our experiments, we considered both practical and theoretical factors related to LLMs. English was chosen for several reasons. First, the vast majority of training data for LLMs is in English (e.g. 93 % in GPT-3, [Brown et al., 2020a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx8)), and their internal representations are therefore primarily shaped by this language [Zhong et al., 2024a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx39); [Schut et al., 2025a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx34). Moreover, most engineering decisions during model development were made with English in mind, including the design of tokenization methods such as Byte Pair Encoding (BPE), which are optimized for English [Zhong et al., 2024a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx39). In this sense, English represents the model’s “default mode.”

For our experiments, we chose the well-established Brown family corpus BE21 [Baker, 2023a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx3). While we cannot rule out the possibility that this corpus was included in the models’ training data, this does not pose a problem: the training data is compressed in such a way that models do not memorize original texts verbatim. Our inspection of generated continuations confirmed this — none of them reproduced the original corpus texts exactly.

#### 2.1.1 Czech

As explained earlier, one of our aims was to examine how models perform in a “smaller” language, that is, one not centrally represented in their training data. In fact, this applies to virtually all languages other than English. According to [Johnson et al., 2022a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx15), as of 2019, the second most represented language in GPT-3’s training data was French (1.8%), followed by German (1.5%). All other languages were represented at below 1%. When studying the language capacities of LLMs, it is therefore crucial to focus on languages other than English, since English occupies a very unique position.

We chose Czech for two practical reasons. First, a pipeline for register analysis developed by Cvrček [Cvrček et al., 2018a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx10) allows us to apply Multidimensional Analysis (MDA) to newly generated AI corpora. Second, we had access to the multi-genre Koditex corpus [Zasina et al., 2018a](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#biba.bibx38), which was used for the original MDA on Czech and therefore provided a well-suited basis for generating comparable AI corpora.

From Koditex, we selected only original Czech texts (excluding translations), omitted poetry, restricted the sample to written texts, and took only one excerpt from each text.

## Appendix A Soft Banning

In real-world use cases, it is often not preferable to ban a word or phrase outright. In these cases, a scalable "soft ban" is preferred, where there is a general suppression effect, but the suppressed vocab may still be used if there are no good alternatives.

An example of how soft-banning works when there are no good alternate candidates:

    We have the word “tapestry” in our banlist, and have set ban-strength = 0.2 and min-p = 0.1.
    
2.  Step 2.
    
    The user requests an essay on tapestry weaving.

    The “Tapestry” token is reduced to probnew\=0.99×10−10⋅0.2\=0.0099\\mathrm{prob}\_{\\text{new}}=0.99\\times 10^{-10\\cdot 0.2}=0.0099.
    
5.  Step 5.
    
    After probability rescaling, min-p still excludes “Mural” from consideration, since 0.00050.0099≈0.05<0.1\\tfrac{0.0005}{0.0099}\\approx 0.05<0.1 (the min-p threshold), resulting in “Tapestry” remaining the only candidate for sampling.

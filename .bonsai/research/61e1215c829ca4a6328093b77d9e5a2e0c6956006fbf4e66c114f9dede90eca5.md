### 1.2 Vocabulary (5 markers)

These markers capture lexical richness and character-level distributional patterning commonly used in stylometric analysis.

Yule’s K

A vocabulary concentration measure defined as:

|  | K=104⋅∑i=1Vfi2−NN2K=10^{4}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{V}f_{i}^{2}-N}{N^{2}} |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where NN is total tokens, VV is vocabulary size, and fif\_{i} is the frequency of the ii\-th word type. Lower values indicate richer, less repetitive vocabulary (Yule, 1944).

Honoré’s R

A hapax-based richness measure defined as:

|  | R=100⋅log⁡N1−V1/VR=100\cdot\frac{\log N}{1-V_{1}/V} |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where NN is total tokens, VV is vocabulary size, and V1V\_{1} is the number of hapax legomena (words appearing exactly once). Higher values indicate greater vocabulary richness (Honoré, 1979).

MTLD

Measure of Textual Lexical Diversity. Higher values indicate greater lexical diversity.

Mean word length

Mean number of characters per token.

Character trigram entropy

Shannon entropy over character trigram distribution:

|  | H=−∑t∈Tp(t)log2p(t)H=-\sum_{t\in T}p(t)\log_{2}p(t) |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where TT is the set of character trigrams and p⁡(t)p(t) is the probability of trigram tt.

## Detailed

### 1.2 Vocabulary (5 markers)

These markers capture lexical richness and character-level distributional patterning commonly used in stylometric analysis.

Yule’s K

A vocabulary concentration measure defined as:

|  | K=104⋅∑i=1Vfi2−NN2K=10^{4}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{V}f_{i}^{2}-N}{N^{2}} |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where NN is total tokens, VV is vocabulary size, and fif\_{i} is the frequency of the ii\-th word type. Lower values indicate richer, less repetitive vocabulary (Yule, 1944). Relatively robust to text length.

Honoré’s R

A hapax-based richness measure defined as:

|  | R=100⋅log⁡N1−V1/VR=100\cdot\frac{\log N}{1-V_{1}/V} |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where NN is total tokens, VV is vocabulary size, and V1V\_{1} is the number of hapax legomena (words appearing exactly once). Higher values indicate greater vocabulary richness (Honoré, 1979).

MTLD

Measure of Textual Lexical Diversity. Computed as the mean length of sequential word strings maintaining a type-token ratio above 0.72. More robust to text length than traditional TTR (McCarthy & Jarvis, 2010). Higher values indicate greater lexical diversity.

Mean word length

Mean number of characters per token. A commonly used stylometric measure (Mendenhall, 1887).

Character trigram entropy

Shannon entropy over character trigram distribution:

|  | H=−∑t∈Tp(t)log2p(t)H=-\sum_{t\in T}p(t)\log_{2}p(t) |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where TT is the set of character trigrams and p⁡(t)p(t) is the probability of trigram tt. Higher values indicate more diverse character-level patterns. Character n-grams can capture sublexical and orthographic patterning and are often less topic-sensitive than word-based features (Stamatatos, 2009).

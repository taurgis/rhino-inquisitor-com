## Appendix B Additional Analyses

Here we report additional analyses and expanded details from analyses in [§ 6].

H4:

In writing tasks where participants consider authentic style to be important, they are more likely to report willingness to use a piece of text they deem to match their style more accurately.

Beyond individualized style, we consider how post-editing labor is distributed among people from various demographic backgrounds who value writing text that communicates this community membership.

H5:

Participants from marginalized communities who want that community membership to come across in their writing will require more post-editing to capture their personal style.

H4 and H5 are discussed in [§ B.3] and [§ B.4] respectively. We confirm that the style of LLM-generated text used in our study is consistent across participants ([§ B.1]) and discuss our choice of style embedding model ([§ B.2]). We provide additional details about our analysis of the reasons why our participants would or would not prefer to post-edit from LLM-generated drafts in future writing ([§ B.6] to Post-Edit ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")). We also explore whether our results in H1 change on tasks where personal style is viewed as somewhat less important ([§ B.5]) and various aspects of participants’ edits including their lexical choices, length, etc ([§ B.7]).

## Appendix B Additional Analyses

Here we report additional analyses and expanded details from analyses in [§ 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6 "6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style"). These results include discussion of three additional preregistered hypotheses.

H4:

In writing tasks where participants consider authentic style to be important, they are more likely to report willingness to use a piece of text they deem to match their style more accurately.

Beyond individualized style, we consider how post-editing labor is distributed among people from various demographic backgrounds who value writing text that communicates this community membership.

H5:

Participants from marginalized communities who want that community membership to come across in their writing will require more post-editing to capture their personal style.

H4 and H5 are discussed in [§ B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS3 "B.3 Humans Perceptions of Stylistic Authenticity vs Usability of Text ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style") and [§ B.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS4 "B.4 Inequities in Effort to Post-Edit to Capture Individual Style Across Demographic Groups ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style") respectively. We confirm that the style of LLM-generated text used in our study is consistent across participants ([§ B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS1 "B.1 LLM-generated Text’s Style is Consistent Between Participants ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")) and discuss our choice of style embedding model ([§ B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS2 "B.2 Style Embedding Model Selection ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")). We provide additional details about our analysis of the reasons why our participants would or would not prefer to post-edit from LLM-generated drafts in future writing ([§ B.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS6 "B.6 Reasons (Not) to Post-Edit ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")). We also explore whether our results in H1 change on tasks where personal style is viewed as somewhat less important ([§ B.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS5 "B.5 Effectiveness of Post-Editing When Personal Style is Less Important ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")) and various aspects of participants’ edits including their lexical choices, length, etc ([§ B.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS7 "B.7 Characterizing Post-Editing Behavior ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")).

## Summary

3\. Client Behavior > 3.1. Handling Resolution Failures

## Compressed

### [3.1.] [Handling Resolution Failures]

If DNS responses are cryptographically protected (e.g., using DNSSEC or TLS \[[DoT]\] \[[DoH]\]) and SVCB resolution fails due to an authentication error, SERVFAIL response, transport error, or timeout, the client SHOULD abandon its attempt to reach the service, even if the client is SVCB-optional.

A SERVFAIL error can occur if the domain is DNSSEC-signed, the recursive resolver is DNSSEC\-validating, and the attacker is between the recursive resolver and the authoritative DNS server.

If the client enforces DNSSEC validation on A/AAAA responses, it SHOULD apply the same validation policy to SVCB.

If DNS responses are not cryptographically protected, clients MAY treat SVCB resolution failure as fatal or nonfatal.[¶]

If the client is unable to complete SVCB resolution due to its chain length limit, the client MUST fall back to the authority endpoint, as if the service's SVCB record did not exist.[¶]

## Detailed

### [3.1.](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1) [Handling Resolution Failures](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#name-handling-resolution-failure)

If DNS responses are cryptographically protected (e.g., using DNSSEC or TLS \[[DoT](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#RFC7858)\] \[[DoH](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#RFC8484)\]) and SVCB resolution fails due to an authentication error, SERVFAIL response, transport error, or timeout, the client SHOULD abandon its attempt to reach the service, even if the client is SVCB-optional. Otherwise, an active attacker could mount a downgrade attack by denying the user access to the SvcParams.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1-1)

A SERVFAIL error can occur if the domain is DNSSEC-signed, the recursive resolver is DNSSEC\-validating, and the attacker is between the recursive resolver and the authoritative DNS server. A transport error or timeout can occur if an active attacker between the client and the recursive resolver is selectively dropping SVCB queries or responses, based on their size or other observable patterns.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1-2)

If the client enforces DNSSEC validation on A/AAAA responses, it SHOULD apply the same validation policy to SVCB. Otherwise, an attacker could defeat the A/AAAA protection by forging SVCB responses that direct the client to other IP addresses.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1-3)

If DNS responses are not cryptographically protected, clients MAY treat SVCB resolution failure as fatal or nonfatal.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1-4)

If the client is unable to complete SVCB resolution due to its chain length limit, the client MUST fall back to the authority endpoint, as if the service's SVCB record did not exist.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-3.1-5)

## Detailed

### 3.3 Task Preparation

LongJudgeBench is designed to assess LLM judges across representative long-form output evaluation tasks. While LLM-as-a-judge has been adopted in many real-world long-form tasks, existing studies remain fragmented, often limited in scale and heterogeneous in data formats, judgment protocols, and evaluation targets. LongJudgeBench consolidates these scattered settings into a unified benchmark, enabling systematic evaluation of judge reliability across diverse long-form scenarios. Specifically, we construct or collect six evaluation datasets from five real-world scenarios: (i) deep research, (ii) scientific survey, (iii) creative writing, (iv) long-chain analysis, and (v) systematic review. These datasets span multiple domains, two languages (Chinese and English), and three judgment protocols: pointwise, pairwise, and listwise evaluation.

Deep research. We first incorporate DeepResearch Bench [Du et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#bib.bib17), which provides expert-designed research questions and long research reports. However, its publicly released human-annotated subset is limited to 200 Chinese reports stored as plain strings, lacking multilingual coverage and omitting presentation-level information such as tables, figures, structured layouts, and other document elements commonly found in real reports. To address these limitations, we further construct and annotate RealDeepResearch, which contains 40 research tasks, 4 prompt styles, and 4 generation models, resulting in 640 long documents. RealDeepResearch covers both Chinese and English documents and preserves the original DOCX format, making it closer to real-world deep research evaluation. The annotations were carried out by expert annotators, who were compensated at a fair rate. The total cost was approximately USD 2,260. Further details are provided in Appendix [C](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#A3 "Appendix C Task Preparation Details ‣ Benchmarking LLM-as-a-Judge for Long-Form Output Evaluation").

Scientific survey. We include SurGE [Su et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#bib.bib18), which provides 41 topics and 164 survey documents with human rankings over structural and content quality.

Creative writing. We use a uniformly sampled subset of WritingPreferenceBench [Ying et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#bib.bib19), which focuses on pairwise preferences between long-form creative responses driven by subjective qualities such as style, creativity, and emotional resonance.

Long-chain analysis. We include VerifyBench [Yan et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#bib.bib20), where judges are given long-form model reasoning analyses and are asked to determine whether the final answer is correct.

Systematic review. We include MA [Xie et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#bib.bib26) for evaluating the insight dimension in clinical systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

Following the formalization in Section [3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#S3.SS2 "3.2 Task Formalization ‣ 3 LongJudgeBench ‣ Benchmarking LLM-as-a-Judge for Long-Form Output Evaluation"), we normalize all datasets into a unified instance format while preserving their original evaluation protocols whenever possible. More details about task preparation can be found in Appendix [C](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629#A3 "Appendix C Task Preparation Details ‣ Benchmarking LLM-as-a-Judge for Long-Form Output Evaluation").

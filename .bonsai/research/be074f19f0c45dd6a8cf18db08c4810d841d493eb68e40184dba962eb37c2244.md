#### Detector Evaluation

Detectors work by taking as input a sequence of tokens and outputting a scalar score. In order to convert this score to a binary prediction, we must select a scalar threshold δ\\delta such that if the score s≥δs\\geq\\delta the sequence is predicted to be machine-generated.

We tune the threshold for each model to yield a 5% false positive rate. In other words, we enforce an error rate on the human-written portion of the dataset of 5% and then report accuracy on the machine-generated portion.

In practical terms, accuracy at a fixed FPR of 5% represents how well each detector identifies machine-generated text while only mis-classifying 5% of human-written text. The cost of a false positive—of mislabeling a human-written text as machine-generated—is high. Evaluating detectors at a fixed FPR allows us to compare detector performance while understanding the potential for harm more fully. Our work is one of the first shared resources to fix and disclose FPR, following the rise of this evaluation paradigm in recent robustness research ([Hans et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib29); [Krishna et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib40); [Soto et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1#bib.bib76)).

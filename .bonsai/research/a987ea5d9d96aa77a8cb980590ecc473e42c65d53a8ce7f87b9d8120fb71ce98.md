### 4.1 Main Results

Table [1] details simulation results. At a high level, every model sees its performance degrade over the course of interaction, with average degradations of 50% across tested models by the end of simulation. Even frontier models (Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude 4.6 Opus, GPT 5.4) degrade documents by 25% on average over 20 interactions.

A per-domain breakdown of end-of-simulation scores (Table [2]) reveals that models are not ready for delegated workflows in the vast majority of domains, with models severely corrupting documents (at least -20% degradation) in 80% of our simulated conditions. The Python domain is an outlier: a majority of tested models (17/19) achieve lossless manipulation, resonating with recent findings on delegated coding workflows ([Pimenova et al., 2025]). The top model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) is designated as ready (RS@20 ≥\\geq 98%) in 11 of 52 domains.

We find that short-term performance (after 2 interactions) is not always predictive of long-horizon performance.

We caution the reader to interpret the absolute scores with respect to the scale of our experimental setting. LLMs are tested in a simulation environment that requires completing work on documents with 3-5k tokens, as well as a distractor context of 8-12k tokens, over the course of 20 interactions.

## Detailed

### 4.1 Main Results

Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4 Results ‣ LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate") details simulation results. At a high level, every model sees its performance degrade over the course of interaction, with average degradations of 50% across tested models by the end of simulation. Even frontier models (Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude 4.6 Opus, GPT 5.4) degrade documents by 25% on average over 20 interactions.

A per-domain breakdown of end-of-simulation scores (Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4.1 Main Results ‣ 4 Results ‣ LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate")) reveals that models are not ready for delegated workflows in the vast majority of domains, with models severely corrupting documents (at least -20% degradation) in 80% of our simulated conditions. The Python domain is an outlier: a majority of tested models (17/19) achieve lossless manipulation, resonating with recent findings on delegated coding workflows ([Pimenova et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib91)). The top model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) is designated as ready (RS@20 ≥\\geq 98%) in 11 of 52 domains.

We find that short-term performance (after 2 interactions) is not always predictive of long-horizon performance. For instance, GPT 5 and Kimi K2.5 achieve near-identical performance after two interactions (91.5 vs. 91.1) but diverge sharply over time (ending at 48.3 vs. 64.1). Conversely, Gemini 3 Flash trails Mistral Large 3 by 6.4 points early on (76.0 vs. 82.4) but overtakes it by end of simulation (35.8 vs. 35.5). In other words, short interaction simulations are insufficient to understand long-horizon LLM performance, validating the importance of benchmarks that simulate extended interactions.

We caution the reader to interpret the absolute scores with respect to the scale of our experimental setting. LLMs are tested in a simulation environment that requires completing work on documents with 3-5k tokens, as well as a distractor context of 8-12k tokens, over the course of 20 interactions. Upcoming subsections use a subset of GPT-family models to study how tool use, document size, interaction length, and distractors affect degradation.

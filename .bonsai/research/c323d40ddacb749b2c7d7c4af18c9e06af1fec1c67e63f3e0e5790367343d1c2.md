## Detailed

### 2.3 Epistemic Modality and Stance in NLP

Computational work on hedging detection has provided a foundation for identifying expressions of uncertainty, particularly within scientific literature [Clausen (2010)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib21); [Medlock and Briscoe (2007)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib23). Hedge cues have been shown to be high-precision markers of uncertainty, though their detection remains highly domain-dependent [Szarvas (2008)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib22); [Li and Zhang (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib18). In the context of LLMs, epistemic stance classification has evolved into the study of honesty alignment, where models are trained to verbalise confidence levels and express uncertainty explicitly [Clark et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib7); [Lee et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib8); [Tao et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768v1#bib.bib6). No existing work correlates the presence of these markers with the rhetorical elaborateness of the surrounding text. Moreover, performed hesitancy is not distinguished from genuine epistemic marking as a functional category.

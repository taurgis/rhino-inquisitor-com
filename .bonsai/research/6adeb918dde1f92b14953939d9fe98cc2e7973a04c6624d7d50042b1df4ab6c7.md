## Detailed

### B.1 Permutation Test for Significance of Diversity Results

To test for the significance of the results on diversity ([Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S5.SS2 "5.2 Results ‣ 5 Does writing with LLMs reduce the overall diversity? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")), since we do not have multiple instances of essay corpora from each setting (Solo, GPT3 and InstructGPT), we employ three permutation tests between each pair of settings. A walkthrough example of the permutation test setup is as follows. We wish to evaluate the significance of the difference between the diversity scores of Solo and InstructGPT, as measured by the clustering of key points ([Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S5.T3 "In 5.2 Results ‣ 5 Does writing with LLMs reduce the overall diversity? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")) or by traditional lossless compression algorithms ([Table 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3.T13 "In Essays written with InstructGPT are more compressible. ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")). We first calculate the statistic (difference between the diversity scores) on the collected corpora of essays. We then take the union of Solo and InstructGPT essays, randomly partition this into two equal sets, and recalculate the statistic on these two permuted sets of essays. This process is then repeated for 10001000 different permutations. We then obtain the p-value of this two-tailed permutation test as the proportion of times the absolute value of the statistic calculated on the permuted data is greater than the statistic calculated on the observed data from the user study. This is then repeated for all pairs out of Solo, GPT3, and InstructGPT. Bold values in the results tables ([Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S5.T3 "In 5.2 Results ‣ 5 Does writing with LLMs reduce the overall diversity? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?"), [Table 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3.T13 "In Essays written with InstructGPT are more compressible. ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")) indicate those instances where the p-value on the permutation test for a setup (InstructGPT) was significant at the 5% level over both other setups (GPT3 and Solo).

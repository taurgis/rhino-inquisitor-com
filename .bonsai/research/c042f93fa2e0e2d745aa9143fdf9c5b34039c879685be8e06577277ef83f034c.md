GPT-3.5-turbo in completion mode had inconsistent results in this area; it performed very well at a sampling temperature of 1, while it had very bad results for the temperature of 0 (second to last in figure [4]).

Figure [8] shows the shifts of texts from davinci-002 relative to the second part of the human corpus across six dimensions. This visualization provides a more fine-grained perspective than Figure [3], as it allows us to examine the positions of individual texts and their genre affiliations. In contrast, Figure [9], which compares the base model GPT-3.5 turbo (X-axis) with its instruction-tuned version (Y-axis), reveals a clear genre effect.

## Detailed

### 4.2 Base versus instruction tuned

#### 4.2.1 English

Now we will focus on one of the main questions of this article, namely, how base models differ from models that have been fine-tuned for instruction following. Interestingly, our analysis indicated that the oldest base model (davinci-002) performed very well, especially on Dimension 3, where most other models failed. This is likely because it was not fine-tuned with reinforcement learning (RLHF in the case of OpenAI, RLAIF for Anthropic) or with instruction tuning, and it was the last model not trained on synthetic data produced by earlier LLMs. However, the best overall performance was achieved by LLaMA 3.1 Completion, which is also a base model. GPT-3.5-turbo in completion mode had inconsistent results in this area; it performed very well at a sampling temperature of 1, while it had very bad results for the temperature of 0 (second to last in figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 4.1.1 English ‣ 4.1 Overall results ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts")). Let us look at the details.

Figure [8](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F8 "Figure 8 ‣ 4.2.1 English ‣ 4.2 Base versus instruction tuned ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts") shows the shifts of texts from davinci-002 relative to the second part of the human corpus across six dimensions. The position of a text in the original corpus is plotted on the X-axis, while its position in the davinci-002 corpus is on the Y-axis. This visualization provides a more fine-grained perspective than Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.1.1 English ‣ 4.1 Overall results ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts"), as it allows us to examine the positions of individual texts and their genre affiliations. For davinci-002, the figure shows that although all texts shifted, the dispersion remained relatively “regular,” without noticeable outliers or strong genre effects. For the first, second, and fourth dimensions, the average shifts reached statistical significance.

Figure 8: Relation between the second parts of human written text chunks and th second parts generated by base model Davinci-002 across all dimensions (English).

In contrast, Figure [9](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F9 "Figure 9 ‣ 4.2.1 English ‣ 4.2 Base versus instruction tuned ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts"), which compares the base model GPT-3.5 turbo (X-axis) with its instruction-tuned version (Y-axis), reveals a clear genre effect. The normalized shifts on the first two dimensions occurred primarily in fiction texts (humor, general, mystery, romance), while press, learned, and miscellaneous texts remained relatively stable. This is an insight we would not have gained without such a fine-grained approach. Genres associated with information production largely remained in the same region, whereas fiction — which is typically located on the opposite pole — became even more skewed.

Figure 9: Relation between the second parts generated by GPT-3.5 turbo in completion mode and the second parts generated by instruction tuned GPT-3.5 turbo (English).

As already noted, Llama 3.1 generally reproduces the profile very well. However, as shown in the scatterplot in Figure [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F10 "Figure 10 ‣ 4.2.1 English ‣ 4.2 Base versus instruction tuned ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts"), the instruction tuned model failed completely on some texts, which are then crowded at very similar levels (around –30 on dimension 1 and around 7.5 on dimension 4).

Figure 10: Relation between the second parts generated by Llama 3.1 Base and the second parts generated by instruction tuned Llama 3.1 (English).

#### 4.2.2 Czech

In Czech, we have only one base model that can be examined in this level of detail — GPT-3.5 Turbo in completion mode — since davinci-003 did not produce reliable data in Czech, nor did Llama (the same holds true for the instruction-tuned version of Llama). This model behaved quite similarly to its chat-mode counterpart in terms of overall vector length; however, in the cluster analysis it did not show affinity with related models and thus appeared more as an outlier. This can also be seen in Figure [11](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10179#S4.F11 "Figure 11 ‣ 4.2.2 Czech ‣ 4.2 Base versus instruction tuned ‣ 4 Results and Discussion ‣ Benchmark of stylistic variation in LLM-generated texts"), where the effect is visible across all dimensions and genres.

Perhaps the most interesting observation for Czech is that, in fact, no truly functional base model exists for this language.

Figure 11: Relation between the second parts generated by GPT-3.5 turbo in completion mode and the second parts generated by instruction tuned GPT-3.5 turbo (Czech).

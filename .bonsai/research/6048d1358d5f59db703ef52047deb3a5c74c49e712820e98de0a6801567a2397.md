### D.1 Soft Watermark Detector

![Refer to caption](2303.11156v4/images/the.png)

Figure 16: Inferred green list score for the token “the”. The plot shows the top 50 words from our set of common words that are likely to be in the green list. The word “first” occurred ∼25%\\sim 25\\% of the time as suffix to “the”.

In [Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib25), they watermark LLM outputs by asserting the model to output tokens with some specific pattern that can be easily detected with meager error rates. Soft watermarked texts are majorly composed of green list tokens. If an adversary can learn the green lists for the soft watermarking scheme, they can use this information to generate human-written texts that are detected to be watermarked. Our experiments show that the soft watermarking scheme can be spoofed efficiently. Though the soft watermarking detector can detect the presence of a watermark very accurately, it cannot be certain if this pattern is actually generated by a human or an LLM. An adversarial human can compose derogatory watermarked texts in this fashion that are detected to be watermarked, which might cause reputational damage to the developers of the watermarked LLM. Therefore, it is important to study spoofing attacks to avoid such scenarios.

In watermarking, the prefix word s(t−1)s^{(t-1)} determines the green list for selecting the word s(t)s^{(t)}. The attacker’s objective is to compute a proxy of green lists for the NN most commonly used words in the vocabulary. We use a small value of N\=181N=181 for our experiments. The attacker queries the watermarked OPT-1.3B [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib57) 10610^{6} times to observe pair-wise token occurrences in its output to estimate green list score for the NN tokens. We find that inputting nonsense sentences composed of the NN common words encourages the LLM to output text mostly composed of these words. This makes the querying more efficient. A token with a high green list score for a prefix s(t)s^{(t)} might be in its green list (see Figure [16](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A4.F16 "Figure 16 ‣ D.1 Soft Watermark Detector ‣ Appendix D More Details on Spoofing ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?")). We build a tool that helps adversarial humans create watermarked sentences by providing them with proxy green list. In this manner, we can spoof watermarking models easily. See Table [15](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A4.T15 "Table 15 ‣ D.1 Soft Watermark Detector ‣ Appendix D More Details on Spoofing ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") for example sentences created by an adversarial human. Figure [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#S3.F7 "Figure 7 ‣ 3 Spoofing Attacks on Generative AI-text Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") shows that the performance of watermark-based detectors degrades significantly in the presence of paraphrasing and spoofing attacks, showing that they are not reliable.

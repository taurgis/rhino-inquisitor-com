### 6.5 (Why) Do Participants Like to Post-Edit?

Here, we present exploratory results on the factors influencing participants’ satisfaction with text written in this study and the process of post-editing. In our post-survey, participants were asked how they would prefer to write text in the future in scenarios where personal style is important. A majority (5858) would prefer to post-edit text as they did in the treatment block, while a large contingency (2020) would prefer to write without AI assistance. A few participants (33) would prefer to use model generations without post-editing. Overall, after participating in the study, participants reported being more likely to use AI in writing tasks where they believe capturing their personal style is important compared to their pre-study preferences (t⁡(80)\=5.54t(80)=5.54, p<.0001p<.0001, g\=0.64g=0.64). Participants generally found writing alone somewhat more mentally demanding (t⁡(80)\=−2.301t(80)=-2.301, p\=.024p=.024, g\=−0.282g=-0.282), time-consuming (t⁡(80)\=−2.214t(80)=-2.214, p\=.03p=.03, g\=−0.260g=-0.260), and effortful (t⁡(80)\=−2.602t(80)=-2.602, p\=.011p=.011, g\=−0.266g=-0.266) than post-editing.

As we discuss in [§ 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS1 "6.1 Effectiveness of Post-Editing in Improving Stylistic Authenticity ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style") and [§ B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#A2.SS3 "B.3 Humans Perceptions of Stylistic Authenticity vs Usability of Text ‣ Appendix B Additional Analyses ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style"), participants agreed with embedding-based measurements of stylistic similarity (which showed an increase in self-similarity after post-editing in [§ 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444#S6.SS1 "6.1 Effectiveness of Post-Editing in Improving Stylistic Authenticity ‣ 6 Results ‣ Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style")) and that increased perceived self-similarity was associated with higher perceived usability. Putting these findings together, we see that participants overall indicated that they believe their post-edited text matches their personal style better (p<.0001p<.0001, g\=0.56g=0.56, 95%95\\% CI: \[0.420.42, 0.690.69\]) and is more usable (p<.0001p<.0001, g\=0.53g=0.53, 95%95\\% CI: \[0.390.39, 0.660.66\]) than the original LLM-generated text.

Participants preferring to write alone ranked the “Originality” of the LLM-generated drafts as a somewhat more important factor than those preferring to post-edit.

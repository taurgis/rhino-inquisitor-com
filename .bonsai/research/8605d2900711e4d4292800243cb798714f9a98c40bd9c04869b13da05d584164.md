## 2 Related work

##### Biases in LLMs outputs

LLM-generated content is known to exhibit a variety of stereotypical biases ([Bender et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib9); [Weidinger et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib89); [Bai et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib6)). In single-turn settings, LLMs perpetrate ([Nadeem et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib63)) or even amplify human biases based on gender, nationality, race, and religion ([Kotek et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib49)). For instance, the GPT model was shown to exhibit cultural values similar to those of WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrial, Rich, Democratic) cultures ([Atari et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib5)). LLMs trained through reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) were found to overly express left-wing opinions on American politics — a tendency that, once formed, is difficult to avoid even after steering the model toward different demographic groups ([Santurkar et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib77)). Finally, a large body of work compared cognitive biases of models to those of humans ([Coda-Forno et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib20); [Liu et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib52); [Binz and Schulz (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib10)).

##### Transmission chains featuring artificial agents

Several studies have applied experimental designs used in cultural evolution to study knowledge and skill accumulation in groups of Reinforcement Learning agents ([Cook et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib21); [Schmitt et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib78); [Team et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib85); [Prystawski et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib70)). Closer to the current study, populations of LLMs have also been studied ([Brinkmann et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib12)). Iterative chains of generative models trained on the preceding model’s output have been shown to sometimes collapse toward the most likely outputs while the tails of the original distribution disappear ([Shumailov et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib81); [Peterson, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib68); [Gerstgrasser et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib34); [Kazdan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib45); [Dohmatob et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib29)). This idea of using LLM-generated content to fine-tune the next generation has also been applied to groups of LLMs with various communication structures ([Helm et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib40)). Similar to our approach, iterative chains with frozen (i.e., not re-trained) LLMs have been shown to express human-like biases in terms of gender stereotypes, positivity, and social, threat, and biology-related information ([Acerbi and Stubbersfield, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib1)). Strong, but non-human-like biases for producing factual information have also been observed ([Chuang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib19)), stressing the importance of understanding the evolution of content in LLMs and the ways it might deviate from human cultural evolution.

##### Iterated Learning

Parallel works (([Ren et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib73); [Zhu and Griffiths, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib98))) have studied similar questions using different methodologies, namely the framework of Bayesian Iterated Learning (BIL) (([Kirby et al., 2014](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib48); [Griffiths and Kalish, 2007](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib36))). Under this framework, attraction is conceptualized as convergence towards priors, whereas we conceptualize it as the fixed point of a recurrent sequence. Therefore, BIL allows to make prediction about how an agents’ hypothesis about a given phenomenon will evolve through social interaction, while our method captures how a specific cultural trait will evolve. Moreover, our framework enables us to avoid assuming that LLMs possess “priors” in the same way that humans do, which might be problematic (see [Kovač et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib50); [Shanahan et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib79)).

##### Evaluation of LLMs in social contexts

While not focusing on the effect of multi-turn interactions, an important body of work has started to study the social cognition of LLMs. These studies revealed that GPT-4 struggles to reach human performance with respect to social commonsense reasoning and strategic communication skills (([Zhou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib97))). In a similar vein, [Huang et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503#bib.bib42) found that manipulating the communication structure can make LLM collectives more resilient to the introduction of malicious agents.

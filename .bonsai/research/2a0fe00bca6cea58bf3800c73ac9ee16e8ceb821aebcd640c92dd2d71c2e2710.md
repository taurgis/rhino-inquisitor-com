### F.1 Methodology

Figure A3: Failure-mode re-aggregation. Each tagged failure instance is classified as deletion or corruption. Deletion share ranges from 28% (Claude 4.6 Opus) to 43% (GPT 5 Chat, GPT 4o).

##### Count-based decomposition.

Our domain-specific evaluators (Figure [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#S2.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 2.2.2 Domain-Specific Evaluation ‣ 2.2 Benchmark Construction ‣ 2 The DELEGATE-52 Benchmark ‣ LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate")) parse documents into structural elements (e.g., ingredients in a recipe, entries in a ledger) and report both reference and generated element counts. Let nrefn\_{\\mathrm{ref}} and ngenn\_{\\mathrm{gen}} be the reference and generated element counts, and s∈\[0,1\]s\\in\[0,1\] the reconstruction score. We define coverage \=min⁡(ngen/nref, 1)\=\\min(n\_{\\mathrm{gen}}/n\_{\\mathrm{ref}},\\,1), which captures the fraction of expected elements that are present. The deletion component is 1−coverage1-\\text{coverage} (elements that are missing), and the corruption component is coverage−s\\text{coverage}-s (elements that are present but incorrect). The two components sum to 1−s1-s, the total degradation. This analysis is conducted on a subset of 38 of the 52 domains for which element counts meaningfully represents the core element in the domain.

##### Failure-mode re-aggregation.

In a separate analysis, we tagged failure instances from our simulation with one of 11 failure-mode labels. We group these labels into two buckets: deletion (content\_loss and truncation) and corruption (the remaining 9 tags: hallucination, structure\_change, skipped\_backward\_edit, syntax\_error, mathematical\_error, duplicated\_content, reordering, templated\_completion, and other).

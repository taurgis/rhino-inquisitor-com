## 6 Why does writing with InstructGPT reduce diversity?

We consistently observe that writing with InstructGPT results in a reduction of content diversity, whereas this is not the case with GPT3 despite users seemingly engaging equally with both models ([Section 3]). First, InstructGPT may produce less diverse text, influencing the essay by directly contributing text. #### InstructGPT generates less diverse text than GPT3.

We first confirm that generations from InstructGPT are less diverse than GPT3, which has also been observed in prior work. In our setting, we measure the diversity of model generation by the pairwise similarity of the five generated continuations upon each user query, similar to self-BLEU ([Zhu et al., 2018]). We observe that InstructGPT indeed generates less diverse text than GPT3 on average (0.200.20 vs 0.110.11 on Rouge-L), with significance at the 5% level using an independent samples tt\-test.1212 12 We also obtain the same conclusion using BertScore as the similarity metric (see [Figure 12] in [Appendix C]). Since users incorporate text from both models at similar rates ([Table 1]), it is plausible that the less diverse suggestions from InstructGPT lead to a decrease in the diversity of the final co-written essay.

## 6 Why does writing with InstructGPT reduce diversity?

We consistently observe that writing with InstructGPT results in a reduction of content diversity, whereas this is not the case with GPT3 despite users seemingly engaging equally with both models ([Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S3 "3 How much do users engage with the model? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")). This raises the question of how writing with InstructGPT makes a difference. There are two potential contributors. First, InstructGPT may produce less diverse text, influencing the essay by directly contributing text. Second, users may write differently in the presence of assistance, e.g., adopting the model’s opinion ([Jakesch et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib21)). In this section, we aim to answer this question by disentangling the effect from the model and the user in collaborative settings.

#### InstructGPT generates less diverse text than GPT3.

We first confirm that generations from InstructGPT are less diverse than GPT3, which has also been observed in prior work. The technical report for GPT4 finds that the feedback-tuned model is less calibrated ([OpenAI, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib32)) and [Bai et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib3) find that finetuning leads to decreased entropy of the output distribution. In our setting, we measure the diversity of model generation by the pairwise similarity of the five generated continuations upon each user query, similar to self-BLEU ([Zhu et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib55)). Thus, higher similarity scores indicate lower diversity. We observe that InstructGPT indeed generates less diverse text than GPT3 on average (0.200.20 vs 0.110.11 on Rouge-L), with significance at the 5% level using an independent samples tt\-test.1212 12 We also obtain the same conclusion using BertScore as the similarity metric (see [Figure 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3.F12 "In InstructGPT presents less diverse suggestions to users than GPT3 ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?") in [Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3 "Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")). Since users incorporate text from both models at similar rates ([Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S3.T1 "In 3 How much do users engage with the model? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")), it is plausible that the less diverse suggestions from InstructGPT lead to a decrease in the diversity of the final co-written essay. Next, we examine the diversity of model-written and user-written text in the essays directly.

Finally, we disentangle the effect from the model and the user for the increase in homogenization observed in [Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S4.F2 "In 4 Does writing with LLMs result in more similar essays? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?"). In [Figure 4(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S6.F4.sf1 "In Figure 4 ‣ InstructGPT generates less diverse text than GPT3. ‣ 6 Why does writing with InstructGPT reduce diversity? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?"), we compare the user-contributed and model-contributed homogenization across the InstructGPT and GPT3 groups. However, we note that user-contributed key points in co-written essays are a subset of the original key points and hence exhibit more variation across essays. Therefore, they are not directly comparable to those in Solo. and the increased homogenization is mainly due to InstructGPT contributions.

#### Takeaway.

Our results suggest that the users’ lexical choice and argument generation is not affected when writing with model assistance. The reduction in diversity in collaborative writing is attributed more to text contributed directly by the InstructGPT model. This paints a relatively optimistic picture as the risk of forming a feedback loop through the mutual influence between the user and the model is potentially limited. However, it is important to note that our study focuses on single interactions between users and models, and the dynamics might change through repeated interactions over time.

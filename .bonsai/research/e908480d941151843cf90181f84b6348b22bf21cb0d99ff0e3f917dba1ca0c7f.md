## Compressed

### [1.2.] [Notational Conventions]

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 \[[RFC2119]\] \[[RFC8174]\] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

The terms "content negotiation" and "status code" are from \[[HTTP]\].

The term "Publisher" refers to an organisation, company, or individual that publishes one or more APIs for use by external third parties. A fictional Publisher named "example" is used throughout this document. The examples use the Fully Qualified Domain Names (FQDNs) "www.example.com", "developer.example.com", "apis.example.com", "apis.example.net", "gaming.example.com", and "iot.example.net", where the .com and .net Top-Level Domains (TLDs) and various subdomains are simply used to illustrate that the "example" Publisher may have their API portfolio distributed across various domains for which they are the authority.

In this document, "API" refers to the specification resources required for an external party (or in the case of "private" APIs, an internal party) to implement software that uses the Publisher's API.[¶]

The specification recommends the use of TLS.

These words may also appear in this document in lower case as plain English words, absent their normative meanings.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#section-1.2-1)

The terms "content negotiation" and "status code" are from \[[HTTP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#RFC9110)\]. The term "well-known URI" is from \[[WELL-KNOWN](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#RFC8615)\]. The term "link relation" is from \[[WEB-LINKING](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#RFC8288)\].[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#section-1.2-2)

The term "Publisher" refers to an organisation, company, or individual that publishes one or more APIs for use by external third parties. A fictional Publisher named "example" is used throughout this document. The examples use the Fully Qualified Domain Names (FQDNs) "www.example.com", "developer.example.com", "apis.example.com", "apis.example.net", "gaming.example.com", and "iot.example.net", where the .com and .net Top-Level Domains (TLDs) and various subdomains are simply used to illustrate that the "example" Publisher may have their API portfolio distributed across various domains for which they are the authority. Scenarios where the Publisher "example" is not the authority for a given _.example._ domain are made explicit in the text.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#section-1.2-3)

In this document, "API" refers to the specification resources required for an external party (or in the case of "private" APIs, an internal party) to implement software that uses the Publisher's API.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#section-1.2-4)

The specification recommends the use of TLS. Hence, "HTTPS" and "https://" are used throughout.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9727/#section-1.2-5)

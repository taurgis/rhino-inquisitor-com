### 5.1 Large Language Models for Judgment

LLMs are widely used in judgment tasks such as response ranking ([Cui et al., 2023]; [Liu et al., 2023a]), reward modeling ([Lee et al., 2023]; [Wu et al., 2024]), and verifying agent task completion ([Qin et al., 2023]; [Xia et al., 2024]), driven by their scalability and cost-effectiveness. Leveraging the inherent knowledge and instruction-following abilities of LLMs, researchers can guide these models to perform judgments by directly integrating rules into the prompts ([Zheng et al., 2023]; [Sun et al., 2023]). To further refine the judgment capabilities of LLMs in areas such as helpfulness and harmlessness ([Bai et al., 2022]; [Wang et al., 2023c]), numerous datasets and models have been developed ([Lambert et al., 2024]; [Wang et al., 2023b]; [Zhu et al., 2023]), greatly advancing the development and democratization of LLM-based judgment.

## Detailed

### 5.1 Large Language Models for Judgment

LLMs are widely used in judgment tasks such as response ranking ([Cui et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib8); [Liu et al., 2023a](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib29)), reward modeling ([Lee et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib22); [Wu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib46)), and verifying agent task completion ([Qin et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib35); [Xia et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib47)), driven by their scalability and cost-effectiveness. Leveraging the inherent knowledge and instruction-following abilities of LLMs, researchers can guide these models to perform judgments by directly integrating rules into the prompts ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib52); [Sun et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib37)). To further refine the judgment capabilities of LLMs in areas such as helpfulness and harmlessness ([Bai et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib2); [Wang et al., 2023c](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib44)), numerous datasets and models have been developed ([Lambert et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib21); [Wang et al., 2023b](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib43); [Zhu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib53)), greatly advancing the development and democratization of LLM-based judgment. Another active research area focuses on the meta-evaluation of LLM judges, examining the alignment between LLM judgments and human assessments ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib52); [Dubois et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib10)), as well as identifying bias in these judges ([Koo et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib20); [Ye et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib50); [Chen et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib5)). In this work, we focus on self-preference bias and propose a novel method to more accurately quantify it in LLMs.

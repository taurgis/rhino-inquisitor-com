## Compressed

### [4.2.] [Recursive Resolvers]

Whether the recursive resolver is aware of SVCB or not, the normal response construction process used for unknown RR types \[[RFC3597]\] generates the Answer section of the response. Recursive resolvers that are aware of SVCB SHOULD help the client to execute the procedure in [Section 3] with minimum overall latency by incorporating additional useful information into the Additional section of the response as follows:[¶]

1.  Incorporate the results of SVCB resolution. If the recursive resolver's local chain length limit (which may be different from the client's limit) has been reached, terminate.[¶]
2.  If any of the resolved SVCB records are in AliasMode, choose one of them at random, and resolve SVCB, A, and AAAA records for its TargetName.[¶]
    
    *   If any SVCB records are resolved, go to Step 1.[¶]
    *   Otherwise, incorporate the results of A and AAAA resolution, and terminate.[¶]
3.  All the resolved SVCB records are in ServiceMode. Resolve A and AAAA queries for each TargetName (or for the owner name if TargetName is "."), incorporate all the results, and terminate.[¶]

In this procedure, "resolve" means the resolver's ordinary recursive resolution procedure, as if processing a query for that RRset. Errors or anomalies in obtaining additional records MAY cause this process to terminate but MUST NOT themselves cause the resolver to send a failure response.[¶]

See [Section 2.4.2] for additional safeguards for recursive resolvers to implement to mitigate loops.[¶]

See [Section 5.2] for possible optimizations of this procedure.[¶]

### [4.2.](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2) [Recursive Resolvers](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#name-recursive-resolvers)

Whether the recursive resolver is aware of SVCB or not, the normal response construction process used for unknown RR types \[[RFC3597](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#RFC3597)\] generates the Answer section of the response. Recursive resolvers that are aware of SVCB SHOULD help the client to execute the procedure in [Section 3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#client-behavior) with minimum overall latency by incorporating additional useful information into the Additional section of the response as follows:[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-1)

1.  Incorporate the results of SVCB resolution. If the recursive resolver's local chain length limit (which may be different from the client's limit) has been reached, terminate.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-2.1)
2.  If any of the resolved SVCB records are in AliasMode, choose one of them at random, and resolve SVCB, A, and AAAA records for its TargetName.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-2.2.1)
    
    *   If any SVCB records are resolved, go to Step 1.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-2.2.2.1)
    *   Otherwise, incorporate the results of A and AAAA resolution, and terminate.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-2.2.2.2)
3.  All the resolved SVCB records are in ServiceMode. Resolve A and AAAA queries for each TargetName (or for the owner name if TargetName is "."), incorporate all the results, and terminate.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-2.3)

In this procedure, "resolve" means the resolver's ordinary recursive resolution procedure, as if processing a query for that RRset. This includes following any aliases that the resolver would ordinarily follow (e.g., CNAME, DNAME \[[DNAME](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#RFC6672)\]). Errors or anomalies in obtaining additional records MAY cause this process to terminate but MUST NOT themselves cause the resolver to send a failure response.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-3)

See [Section 2.4.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#alias-mode) for additional safeguards for recursive resolvers to implement to mitigate loops.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-4)

See [Section 5.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#incomplete-response) for possible optimizations of this procedure.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2-5)

#### [4.2.1.](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2.1) [DNS64](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#name-dns64)

DNS64 resolvers synthesize responses to AAAA queries for names that only have an A record ([Section 5.1.7](https://rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6147#section-5.1.7) of \[[RFC6147](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#RFC6147)\]). SVCB-aware DNS64 resolvers SHOULD apply the same synthesis logic when resolving AAAA records for the TargetName for inclusion in the Additional section (Step 2 in [Section 4.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#recursive-behavior)) and MAY omit the A records from this section.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2.1-1)

DNS64 resolvers MUST NOT extrapolate the AAAA synthesis logic to the IP hints in the SvcParams ([Section 7.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#svcparamkeys-iphints)). Modifying the IP hints would break DNSSEC validation for the SVCB record and would not improve performance when the above recommendation is implemented.[¶](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9460/#section-4.2.1-2)

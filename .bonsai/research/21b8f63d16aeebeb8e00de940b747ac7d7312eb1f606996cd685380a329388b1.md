### 2.2 Combining excess words puts a lower bound on LLM usage

The unprecedented increase in excess style words in 2024 allows to use them as markers of LLM usage. Each frequency gap δ\\delta for a single marker word gives a lower bound on the fraction of abstracts that went through LLMs in 2024.

For a given set of marker words GG, we computed its frequency gap ΔG\=PG−QG\\Delta\_{G}=P\_{G}-Q\_{G} where PGP\_{G} and QGQ\_{G} are the observed and the expected fraction of abstracts containing at least one of the words from GG. To avoid a computationally expensive combinatorial search for a set of words with the highest ΔG\\Delta\_{G}, we used two independent heuristics to form two separate sets, focusing on either rare or common style excess words.

To create the rare set, we grouped all 2024 excess style words with frequency p<Tp<T and computed the frequency gap Δrare\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}} as a function of threshold TT (Figure [4]). The frequency gap was Δrare\=0.136\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}}=0.136, putting the lower bound on the LLM usage in 2024 at 13.6% (Figure [5]a). Importantly, this is only a lower bound because some of the abstracts that did go through an LLM may fail to contain any of the style words we selected here (see Discussion).

To create the common set, we grouped together excess style words with high individual δ\\delta values, manually adjusting the selection to maximize the frequency gap Δcommon\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}. This set yielded a very similar frequency gap as the rare words: Δcommon\=0.134\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}=0.134 (Figure [5]a).

As the rare and the common sets were non-overlapping, both serve as an independent estimate of the lower bound. Averaging the two estimates, we obtained Δ\=(Δcommon+Δrare)/2\=0.135\\Delta=(\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}+\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}})/2=0.135 as our final estimate of the lower bound of LLM usage (13.5%).

For comparison, using the set of four excess content words from 2021, covid, pandemic, coronavirus, and sars, yielded frequency gap Δ\=0.069\\Delta=0.069.

### 2.2 Combining excess words puts a lower bound on LLM usage

The unprecedented increase in excess style words in 2024 allows to use them as markers of LLM usage. Each frequency gap δ\\delta for a single marker word gives a lower bound on the fraction of abstracts that went through LLMs in 2024. For example, δ\=0.052\\delta=0.052 for the LLM style marker word potential means that in 2024 there were 5.2 percentage points more abstracts containing that word than expected based on the 2021–2022 data, suggesting that at least 5.2% of all abstracts in 2024 went through an LLM. We reasoned that combining multiple marker words together can increase the lower bound.

For a given set of marker words GG, we computed its frequency gap ΔG\=PG−QG\\Delta\_{G}=P\_{G}-Q\_{G} where PGP\_{G} and QGQ\_{G} are the observed and the expected fraction of abstracts containing at least one of the words from GG. As above, we interpret ΔG\\Delta\_{G} as a lower bound on the LLM usage. Note that this does not assume independence between words in GG. To avoid a computationally expensive combinatorial search for a set of words with the highest ΔG\\Delta\_{G}, we used two independent heuristics to form two separate sets, focusing on either rare or common style excess words.

To create the rare set, we grouped all 2024 excess style words with frequency p<Tp<T and computed the frequency gap Δrare\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}} as a function of threshold TT (Figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5#S2.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 2.1 Excess words indicate widespread LLM usage ‣ 2 Results ‣ Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary")). We obtained the highest Δrare\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}} value with T\=0.02T=0.02, resulting in a set of 291 words (Figure [S6](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5#Sx3.F6 "Figure S6 ‣ Supplementary Figures ‣ Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary")). The frequency gap was Δrare\=0.136\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}}=0.136, putting the lower bound on the LLM usage in 2024 at 13.6% (Figure [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5#S2.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 2.1 Excess words indicate widespread LLM usage ‣ 2 Results ‣ Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary")a). Importantly, this is only a lower bound because some of the abstracts that did go through an LLM may fail to contain any of the style words we selected here (see Discussion).

To create the common set, we grouped together excess style words with high individual δ\\delta values, manually adjusting the selection to maximize the frequency gap Δcommon\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}. This led to the following set of 10 words: across, additionally, comprehensive, crucial, enhancing, exhibited, insights, notably, particularly, within. This set yielded a very similar frequency gap as the rare words: Δcommon\=0.134\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}=0.134 (Figure [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5#S2.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ 2.1 Excess words indicate widespread LLM usage ‣ 2 Results ‣ Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary")a).

As the rare and the common sets were non-overlapping, both serve as an independent estimate of the lower bound. Averaging the two estimates, we obtained Δ\=(Δcommon+Δrare)/2\=0.135\\Delta=(\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{common}}+\\Delta\_{\\mathrm{rare}})/2=0.135 as our final estimate of the lower bound of LLM usage (13.5%).

For comparison, using the set of four excess content words from 2021, covid, pandemic, coronavirus, and sars, yielded frequency gap Δ\=0.069\\Delta=0.069. This shows that the LLM usage in 2024 was at least two times higher than the size of Covid-related literature in 2021.

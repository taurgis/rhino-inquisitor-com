### 3.1 Experimental Setup

#### Models and Datasets.

We select GPT-4o-mini ([Hurst et al., 2024]), Gemini-1.5-Flash ([Team et al., 2024a]), and DeepSeek-V3 ([Liu et al., 2024]) as gold judge models due to their strong judging capabilities. To avoid preference leakage, we choose models of different types from the gold judge models to test self-preference bias. Specifically, we select Llama-3.1-8B(-Instruct), Llama-3.1-70B(-Instruct) ([Grattafiori et al., 2024]), Qwen2.5-7B(-Instruct), Qwen2.5-72B(-Instruct) ([Yang et al., 2024]), and gemma-2-9B(-it) ([Team et al., 2024b]), where "-Instruct" and "-it" indicate models that have undergone post-training. We also discuss proprietary models in Appendix [A.1].

We conduct our experiments on three widely-used datasets: AlpacaEval ([Li et al., 2023]), WMT19 (de-en) ([Foundation, 2019]) and TruthfulQA ([Lin et al., 2021]).

#### Implementation Details.

For all models, we set the temperature to 00 to ensure output determinism and consistency. Given an LLM and two responses, where one response is generated by the LLM itself, we evaluate the two responses using a pairwise comparison approach.

We denote each input to the judge model as (p,rA,rB)(p,r\_{A},r\_{B}), where pp is the judge prompt. This prompt instructs the LLM to judge which of rAr\_{A} and rBr\_{B} is better and to output only token A or B. We collect and normalize the probabilities corresponding to the output tokens A and B. To mitigate the impact of position bias ([Zheng et al., 2023]; [Ye et al., 2024]) on the evaluation results, we swap the order of the responses and compute the average probability for each response across both positions ([Panickssery et al., 2024]). For gold judgments, since some models do not provide output probabilities, we assign a probability of 1.01.0 to the output token from gold judge models and 0.00.0 to the other token. Then, we select the winner by averaging the probabilities of all three gold judge models.

## Detailed

### 3.1 Experimental Setup

#### Models and Datasets.

We select GPT-4o-mini ([Hurst et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib18)), Gemini-1.5-Flash ([Team et al., 2024a](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib38)), and DeepSeek-V3 ([Liu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib28)) as gold judge models due to their strong judging capabilities. To avoid preference leakage, we choose models of different types from the gold judge models to test self-preference bias. Specifically, we select Llama-3.1-8B(-Instruct), Llama-3.1-70B(-Instruct) ([Grattafiori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib13)), Qwen2.5-7B(-Instruct), Qwen2.5-72B(-Instruct) ([Yang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib49)), and gemma-2-9B(-it) ([Team et al., 2024b](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib39)), where "-Instruct" and "-it" indicate models that have undergone post-training. We also discuss proprietary models in Appendix [A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#A1.SS1 "A.1 Self-Preference of Proprietary Models ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments").

We conduct our experiments on three widely-used datasets: AlpacaEval ([Li et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib25)), WMT19 (de-en) ([Foundation, 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib11)) and TruthfulQA ([Lin et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib27)). Following prior work on multi-objective alignment ([Cui et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib8); [Guo et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib15)), we evaluate helpfulness on AlpacaEval and WMT19 (de-en), and truthfulness on TruthfulQA. To facilitate experiments and ensure reliable evaluation, we randomly sample 500 examples from each dataset.

#### Implementation Details.

For all models, we set the temperature to 00 to ensure output determinism and consistency. For pre-trained LLMs, we leverage the in-context learning method ([Brown et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib4)) and prepend two examples to the prompt, enabling them to generate judgments. Given an LLM and two responses, where one response is generated by the LLM itself, we evaluate the two responses using a pairwise comparison approach. Compared to single-response scoring methods, the pairwise comparison approach yields more stable evaluation results ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib52)).

We denote each input to the judge model as (p,rA,rB)(p,r\_{A},r\_{B}), where pp is the judge prompt. This prompt instructs the LLM to judge which of rAr\_{A} and rBr\_{B} is better and to output only token A or B. We collect and normalize the probabilities corresponding to the output tokens A and B. To mitigate the impact of position bias ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib52); [Ye et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib50)) on the evaluation results, we swap the order of the responses and compute the average probability for each response across both positions ([Panickssery et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib33)). Finally, we select the response with the highest average probability as the winner and calculate the win rate over all instructions. The consistency between the theoretical analysis and the empirical implementation is discussed in Appendix [A.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#A1.SS4 "A.4 Consistency Between the Theoretical Bias Estimator and Implementation ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments"). For gold judgments, since some models do not provide output probabilities, we assign a probability of 1.01.0 to the output token from gold judge models and 0.00.0 to the other token. Then, we select the winner by averaging the probabilities of all three gold judge models. Furthermore, we alleviate the influence of length bias by constraining the maximum length of the responses. The detailed prompts are presented in Appendix [A.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#A1.SS6 "A.6 Prompt ‣ Appendix A Appendix ‣ Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments").

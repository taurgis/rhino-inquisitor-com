### Canned emphasis on notability, attribution, and media coverage

*   [WP:AIATTR]

| --- | --- |

Similarly, LLMs act as if the best way to prove that a subject is notable is to hit readers over the head with claims of notability, often by detailing sources that a subject has been covered in and specifying what kind of sources they are (e.g., trade publications, regional media, etc).

This sign is narrow and distinct from human-written press releases, which have of course also cited news clippings for decades. When LLMs are asked to write a Wikipedia article, the result often echoes the exact wording of [Wikipedia's guidelines] like "independent coverage," as if the LLM has collapsed the context between the edit and its justification. [\[c\]]

In articles about people or entities that use social media, LLMs will often note that they "maintain an active social media presence" or something similar. This wording is particularly idiosyncratic to AI text and relatively uncommon on Wikipedia before ~2024.

### Canned emphasis on notability, attribution, and media coverage

Similarly, LLMs act as if the best way to prove that a subject is notable is to hit readers over the head with claims of notability, often by detailing sources that a subject has been covered in and specifying what kind of sources they are (e.g., trade publications, regional media, etc). They often inaccurately attribute their own [superficial analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing#Superficial_analyses) to the source. This is more common in text from AI tools released in 2025 or later.

This sign is narrow and distinct from human-written press releases, which have of course also cited news clippings for decades. The distinctions are the idiosyncratic phrasing above and the fact that the focus is placed on characteristics of _the sources themselves_: their contents, their availability/unavailability in public searching, and their classification in relation to the media landscape and the article's subject. When LLMs are asked to write a Wikipedia article, the result often echoes the exact wording of [Wikipedia's guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:N "Wikipedia:N") like "independent coverage," as if the LLM has collapsed the context between the edit and its justification. [\[c\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing#cite_note-14)

In articles about people or entities that use social media, LLMs will often note that they "maintain an active social media presence" or something similar. This wording is particularly idiosyncratic to AI text and relatively uncommon on Wikipedia before ~2024.

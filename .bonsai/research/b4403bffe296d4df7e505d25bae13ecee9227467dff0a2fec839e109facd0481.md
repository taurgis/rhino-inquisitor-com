## Summary

4 Does writing with LLMs result in more similar essays? > 4.2 Results

## Compressed

### 4.2 Results

#### Writing with InstructGPT produces more similar content.

The corpus homogenization scores when comparing essays from the three groups at the key point level using Rouge-L are 0.15360.1536 for Solo, 0.15780.1578 for GPT3 and 0.16600.1660 for InstructGPT. InstructGPT exhibit higher corpus homogenization than both other groups to a statistically significant degree as verified with p<0.05p<0.05 on an independent samples tt\-test. [Figure 2] shows the homogenization scores across topics at the key point level using Rouge-L.66 6 [Figure 8] and [Figure 9] in [Appendix C] contain similar plots of the homogenization scores at the essay level and key point level using both Rouge-L and BertScore. We observe that the InstructGPT group has a higher median homogenization than other groups on 77 out of 1010 topics.

#### Writing with GPT3 does not increase homogenization.

Although GPT3 and InstructGPT contribute a similar amount of text and key points to the co-written essays ([Section 3]), unlike InstructGPT, GPT3 does not appear to increase the similarity between essays written by different users. In particular, there is no statistically significant difference between the corpus homogenization score of GPT3 and that of Solo at the 5% significance level using an independent samples tt\-test.

#### Writing with InstructGPT produces more similar content.

The corpus homogenization scores when comparing essays from the three groups at the key point level using Rouge-L are 0.15360.1536 for Solo, 0.15780.1578 for GPT3 and 0.16600.1660 for InstructGPT. This result holds across both similarity metrics as well as when the essays are compared at the essay level ([Table 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3.T10 "In Writing with InstructGPT results in more similar content at the key point and raw essay level ‣ Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?") in [Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A3 "Appendix C Additional Results ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")). We observe that the InstructGPT group has a higher median homogenization than other groups on 77 out of 1010 topics. While the effect of model intervention varies across topics, the trend of increased homogenization persists ([Section B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#A2.SS3 "B.3 Significance Testing for Results on Homogenization ‣ Appendix B Significance Testing ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")).

#### Writing with GPT3 does not increase homogenization.

Although GPT3 and InstructGPT contribute a similar amount of text and key points to the co-written essays ([Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#S3 "3 How much do users engage with the model? ‣ Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?")), unlike InstructGPT, GPT3 does not appear to increase the similarity between essays written by different users. In particular, there is no statistically significant difference between the corpus homogenization score of GPT3 and that of Solo at the 5% significance level using an independent samples tt\-test. This discrepancy between InstructGPT and GPT3 suggests that the impact of LLMs on content homogenization is not uniform. While InstructGPT often outperforms GPT3 on benchmarks across a wide range of metrics ([Liang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3#bib.bib26)), our results highlight that this performance boost may come at the cost of creating more homogeneous content in interactive settings.

### 3.2 Fine-Tuning Results

For both detection and comparison tasks in the pairwise setting, ordering bias leads to the average probabilities being the only quantitative distinction in cases where the model favors the second option presented for both orderings of a pairwise question.

Fine-tuning on the self-recognition task causes models to become more confident in their pairwise judgments (Figure [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076#S3.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ 3.2 Fine-Tuning Results ‣ 3 Measuring Correlation between Self-Preference and Self-Recognition ‣ LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations")). When plotting self-recognition score against self-preference, the data clump more tightly around 0.50.5 and the high- and low-score corners.

![Refer to caption](2404.13076v1/images/confidence_density_distribution.png)

Figure 6: GPT-3.5 pairwise results on XSUM after 0, 10, and 500 fine-tuning examples show a pattern of increasing absolute confidence scores.

In the pairwise setting, fine-tuning for self-recognition results in a strong linear correlation between a model’s self-recognition ability and it’s self-preference (Figure [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076#S3.F7 "Figure 7 ‣ 3.2 Fine-Tuning Results ‣ 3 Measuring Correlation between Self-Preference and Self-Recognition ‣ LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations")). We find that fine-tuning for self-recognition ability on one dataset transfers to the other.

Figure 7: Fine-tuning evaluators for self-recognition results across models and datasets results in a linear trend connecting evaluator self-recognition to self-preference. This effect persists when fine-tuning on one dataset and measuring results on the other dataset.

In additional to analyzing the relationship between self-recognition ability and overall dataset self-preference, we measure the correlation between these two properties on the example level (Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.2 Fine-Tuning Results ‣ 3 Measuring Correlation between Self-Preference and Self-Recognition ‣ LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations")). For GPT-3.5 on the XSUM dataset, the evaluator prior to fine-tuning has a correlation of 0.410.41 (Kendall’s τ\\tau) between correctly recognizing its summary from a pair and preferring its summary from that same pair. Every fine-tuning configuration we employ results in a model with a positive correlation on this metric and this correlation does not change meaningfully as self-recognition ability increases.

## 3\. DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS TO IMPROVE AI WRITING

“The secret to good writing is good editing.It’s what separates hastily written, randomly punctuated, incoherent rants from learned polemics and op-eds, and cringe-worthy fan fiction from a critically acclaimed novel ” ([Guinness, 2024]). In this section, we outline the design principles and desiderata that guided our approach to improving AI writing through textual edits.

##### Design Principle 1: Develop a comprehensive edit taxonomy grounded in expert writing practices

This principle emphasizes creating a comprehensive taxonomy of edit categories ([Faigley and Witte, 1981]) rooted in an expert editor and writer’s practices. Grounding the taxonomy in expert writing practices ensures that the edits align with the standards of high-quality writing and are acceptable to its readers. Finally, this principle also acknowledges the complexity of the editing process, recognizing that different categories of edits may be required at various levels of the text, from sentence-level corrections to broader structural changes ([Hayes et al., 1987]; [Sommers, 1980]; [Yang et al., 2017]).

##### Design Principle 2: Leverage edits to balance both meaning preservation and substantive semantic changes

Preserving the core meaning and intent of the original text is crucial to maintaining coherence and faithfulness to the initial ideas. On the other hand, introducing substantive semantic changes is often required to adhere to the quality and characteristics of good writing. Prior work on edit taxonomies focuses on low-level syntactic operations ([Faigley and Witte, 1981]) or semantic edits ([Yang et al., 2017]; [Daxenberger and Gurevych, 2013]; [Laban et al., 2023b]) tailored to specific websites like Wikipedia. LLM-generated text often benefits from syntactic edits. Consequently, semantic edits (both meaning preserving and changing) in AI writing are important for enhancing specificity or reducing unnecessary flourishes and clichés that can otherwise obscure meaning. Our methodology aims to navigate the tension between maintaining original meaning and introducing necessary improvements to mitigate AI-specific writing quirks.

##### Design Principle 3: Utilize edits as a mechanism for enhancing human-AI alignment in writing

Current AI writing systems are developed using pre-trained language models (LMs) refined through human interaction, employing supervised learning and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Following recent work in preference learning ([Dubey et al., 2024]; [D’Oosterlinck et al., 2024]), we evaluate edits as a mechanism for enhancing alignment. ## 3\. DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS TO IMPROVE AI WRITING

“The secret to good writing is good editing.It’s what separates hastily written, randomly punctuated, incoherent rants from learned polemics and op-eds, and cringe-worthy fan fiction from a critically acclaimed novel ” ([Guinness, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib47)). In this section, we outline the design principles and desiderata that guided our approach to improving AI writing through textual edits.

##### Design Principle 1: Develop a comprehensive edit taxonomy grounded in expert writing practices

This principle emphasizes creating a comprehensive taxonomy of edit categories ([Faigley and Witte, 1981](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib34)) rooted in an expert editor and writer’s practices. Prior work has shown that experts and novices define revising in very different ways with experts attending more systematically to different aspects of the text than novices ([Bracewell et al., 1978](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib10); [Sommers, 1980](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib107); [Tyler et al., 1982](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib114); [Rosson, 1983](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib101)). By developing such a taxonomy, we aim to provide an approach to analyzing and enhancing LLM-generated text. It also allows for a more granular understanding of the specific areas where AI writing may fall short and enables targeted improvements. [Sommers (1980)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib107) found that “experienced writers have a second objective; a concern for their readership”. Grounding the taxonomy in expert writing practices ensures that the edits align with the standards of high-quality writing and are acceptable to its readers. Finally, this principle also acknowledges the complexity of the editing process, recognizing that different categories of edits may be required at various levels of the text, from sentence-level corrections to broader structural changes ([Hayes et al., 1987](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib48); [Sommers, 1980](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib107); [Yang et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib116)).

##### Design Principle 2: Leverage edits to balance both meaning preservation and substantive semantic changes

Preserving the core meaning and intent of the original text is crucial to maintaining coherence and faithfulness to the initial ideas. On the other hand, introducing substantive semantic changes is often required to adhere to the quality and characteristics of good writing. Prior work on edit taxonomies focuses on low-level syntactic operations ([Faigley and Witte, 1981](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib34)) or semantic edits ([Yang et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib116); [Daxenberger and Gurevych, 2013](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib23); [Laban et al., 2023b](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib63)) tailored to specific websites like Wikipedia. LLM-generated text often benefits from syntactic edits. These edits (primarily meaning preserving) enhance readability by diversifying sentence structures, expanding vocabulary choices, and minimizing repetitive phrasing. Consequently, semantic edits (both meaning preserving and changing) in AI writing are important for enhancing specificity or reducing unnecessary flourishes and clichés that can otherwise obscure meaning. Our methodology aims to navigate the tension between maintaining original meaning and introducing necessary improvements to mitigate AI-specific writing quirks.

##### Design Principle 3: Utilize edits as a mechanism for enhancing human-AI alignment in writing

Current AI writing systems are developed using pre-trained language models (LMs) refined through human interaction, employing supervised learning and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) ([Ziegler et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib122)) is a key approach, transforming human input into training data to guide LMs toward desired outcomes. The most common type of feedback used with RLHF is binary preferences between pairs of examples sampled from one or more Language Models ([Christiano et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib20)). However, a learned preference ordering can fail to converge to the true one when the desirability of examples depends on noise ([Gao et al., 2024a](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib43)). Following recent work in preference learning ([Dubey et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib31); [D’Oosterlinck et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509#bib.bib26)), we evaluate edits as a mechanism for enhancing alignment.

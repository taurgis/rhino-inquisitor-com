In this section, we analyze the retrieval-based detector proposed in [Krishna et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib27). We show that our recursive paraphrasing attack is effective in breaking their detector. We use the 11B parameter DIPPER paraphraser ([Krishna et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib27)) for our attack. OPT-13B is used to measure the perplexity scores in all the settings.

Figure [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.F13 "Figure 13 ‣ A.4 Retrieval-based Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") shows the performance of the retrieval-based detector in multiple settings. In all the settings, the detection accuracy drops as rounds of recursive paraphrasing proceed with only a slight degradation in perplexity scores. We observe that the detector works well after a single round of paraphrasing (pp1). However, after five rounds of paraphrasing, Best of ppi reduces the detector’s accuracy to close to 50%50\\% with only a slight degradation in perplexity scores. We also find that we can easily spoof the retrieval-based detector as discussed in §[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#S3 "3 Spoofing Attacks on Generative AI-text Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") to deteriorate the detector’s performance to 0%0\\%. Note that retrieval-based detectors are concerning since they might lead to serious privacy issues from storing users’ LLM conversations.

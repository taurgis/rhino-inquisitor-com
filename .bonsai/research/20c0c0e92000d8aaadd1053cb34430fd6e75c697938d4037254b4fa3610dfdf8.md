## Detailed

### 3.3 Linguistic Markers

The study measures normalization using 13 markers selected from computational stylistics and register studies. Selection proceeded in two stages. First, candidate features were identified from the stylometric literature ([Stamatatos, 2009](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib8); [Eder et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib10); [Burrows, 2002](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib34)) and register studies ([Biber, 1988](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib35); [Biber and Conrad, 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib12)). Second, features that were redundant, sparse in short personal narratives, or lacked clear interpretive payoff for questions of voice and authenticity were excluded. The resulting 13 markers span four categories.

*   •
    
    Function word ratio: proportion of tokens that are function words.
    
*   •
    
    MFW coverage: proportion of text covered by each text’s 50 most frequent words.

*   •
    
    Mean word length ([Mendenhall, 1887](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib40)): index of vocabulary complexity.
    
*   •
    
    Character trigram entropy: diversity of character-level patterns, encoding morphological habits and word boundaries while remaining topic-invariant ([Stamatatos, 2009](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib8); [Stamatatos, 2013](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142#bib.bib11)).

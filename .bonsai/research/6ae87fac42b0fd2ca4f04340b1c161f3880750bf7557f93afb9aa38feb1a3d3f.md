## Detailed

### A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors

In this section, we analyze the zero-shot and trained detectors in prior literature ([Mitchell et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib35); [Solaiman et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib48); [Gehrmann et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib16); [Ippolito et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib20); [OpenAI, 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib37)). We use the T5-based paraphraser ([Damodaran, 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib11)), Parrot, to paraphrase the AI-generated text and use OPT-13B to measure the perplexity scores for all the settings. We perform our experiments on the XSum ([Narayan et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib36)), PubMedQA ([Jin et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib22)), and Kafkai ([Kafkai, 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib23)) datasets with GPT-2-Medium and OPT-1.3B as the target generative models. In Figure [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.F12 "Figure 12 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") (ROC curves) and Tables [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") (TPR@1%1\\%FPR values) and [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") (AUROC scores), we present our results. 1d, 1z, 10d, and 10z in Tables [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") and [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.T6 "Table 6 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") refer to different variants of the DetectGPT ([Mitchell et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#bib.bib35)).

Figure [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11156v4#A1.F12 "Figure 12 ‣ A.3 Zero-shot and Trained Detectors ‣ Appendix A Experiments with More Datasets and Models ‣ Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?") shows the performance of various zero-shot and trained detectors in multiple settings. The performance of these detectors drops significantly when the AI-generated text is paraphrased, and when given 5 queries to the detector, an adversary can fool most detectors effectively. Some detectors like OpenAI’s RoBERTa-based models are more resilient on datasets like XSum, but are not reliable on other datasets like Kafkai. The perplexity scores of the GPT-2 generated text before any paraphrasing were 15.58 for XSum, 12.80 for PubMedQA, 19.11 for Kafkai, while the perplexity of OPT-1.3B generated text was 9.31. After paraphrasing, the perplexity scores are 20.06, 16.45, 20.01, and 13.96, respectively.

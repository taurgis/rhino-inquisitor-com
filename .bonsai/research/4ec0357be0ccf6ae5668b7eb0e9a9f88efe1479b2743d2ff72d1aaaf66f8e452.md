## 2 The DBG Score: Measuring Self-Preference in Judge Models

Self-preference, also known as self-enhancement, refers to the tendency of an LLM to favor its own generated responses when making judgments ([Zheng et al., 2023]).

Let SA​(r)S\_{A}(r) denote the score assigned by judge AA to response rr.

|  | ℙ⁡(rA≻rB∣x)=σ⁡(SA​(rA)−SA​(rB)),\mathbb{P}(r_{A}\succ r_{B}\mid x)=\sigma(S_{A}(r_{A})-S_{A}(r_{B})), |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where σ\\sigma is the sigmoid function. Assume that each response rr has an underlying true quality Q⁡(r)Q(r), and that judge AA has an inherent bias bA​(r)b\_{A}(r) toward response rr.

|  | ℙ⁡(rA≻rB∣x)=σ⁡(δ+bA),\mathbb{P}(r_{A}\succ r_{B}\mid x)=\sigma(\delta+b_{A}), |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where δ\=Q⁡(rA)−Q⁡(rB)\\delta=Q(r\_{A})-Q(r\_{B}) captures the quality gap between the two responses, and bA\=bA​(rA)−bA​(rB)b\_{A}=b\_{A}(r\_{A})-b\_{A}(r\_{B}) reflects the bias of judge AA. In the self-preference bias case, we assume that the judge exhibits bias only toward its own response, such that bA​(rB)\=0b\_{A}(r\_{B})=0 and bA\=bA​(rA)\>0b\_{A}=b\_{A}(r\_{A})>0.

|  | wA=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ+bA)].w_{A}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta+b_{A})]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

In contrast, for an unbiased gold judge, the expected preference probability of selecting rAr\_{A} is

|  | w∗=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ)].w^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta)]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

Recent work adopts metrics based on wAw\_{A} to quantify self-preference bias ([Panickssery et al., 2024]; [Ye et al., 2024]). However, this approach conflates the quality of the responses with the self-preference bias of the judge model ([Chen et al., 2025]), leading to biased estimations. Specifically, when models AA and BB correspond to a strong LLM (e.g., GPT-4o ([Hurst et al., 2024])) and a weaker LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct ([Grattafiori et al., 2024])), it becomes ambiguous whether a higher wAw\_{A} is driven by inherent differences in response quality or by the self-preference bias of the judge model AA.

To isolate the self-preference bias of model AA, we propose using the difference between the biased judge and the gold judge as a metric (referred to as the DBG score) for measuring self-preference bias:

|  | w^A=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ+bA)−σ⁡(δ)].\hat{w}_{A}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta+b_{A})-\sigma(\delta)]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

This formulation removes the confounding effect of response quality (captured by δ\\delta) and focuses explicitly on the self-preference bias term bAb\_{A}.

When bAb\_{A} is small, a first-order Taylor approximation yields

|  | w^A≈𝔼x​[σ′​(δ)⋅bA].\hat{w}_{A}\approx\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma^{\prime}(\delta)\cdot b_{A}]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

Assuming a weak correlation between response quality gaps and self-preference bias of AA, we have

|  | w^A≈𝔼x​[σ′​(δ)]⋅𝔼x​[bA],\hat{w}_{A}\approx\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma^{\prime}(\delta)]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{x}[b_{A}], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

suggesting that w^A\\hat{w}\_{A} serves as a linearly scaled estimator of the true bias.

In practice, we aggregate the judgment results from three strong LLMs to construct the unbiased gold judgment:

|  | w^∗=𝔼x,k​[σ⁡(δ+bk)],\hat{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x,k}[\sigma(\delta+b_{k})], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where bkb\_{k} denotes the bias of model kk toward rAr\_{A}.

|  | w^∗=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ)]+𝔼x,k​[Δ],\hat{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta)]+\mathbb{E}_{x,k}[\Delta], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where Δ\\Delta represents the remainder term.

## 2 The DBG Score: Measuring Self-Preference in Judge Models

Self-preference, also known as self-enhancement, refers to the tendency of an LLM to favor its own generated responses when making judgments ([Zheng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib52)). Formally, let AA and BB denote two different LLMs, and let rAr\_{A} and rBr\_{B} represent the responses generated by AA and BB, respectively, in response to the same instruction xx. For simplicity, we focus our analysis on the scenario where model AA serves as the judge.

Let SA​(r)S\_{A}(r) denote the score assigned by judge AA to response rr. Following the Bradley-Terry model ([Bradley and Terry, 1952](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib3)), the probability that judge AA prefers rAr\_{A} over rBr\_{B} is given by:

|  | ℙ⁡(rA≻rB∣x)=σ⁡(SA​(rA)−SA​(rB)),\mathbb{P}(r_{A}\succ r_{B}\mid x)=\sigma(S_{A}(r_{A})-S_{A}(r_{B})), |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where σ\\sigma is the sigmoid function. Assume that each response rr has an underlying true quality Q⁡(r)Q(r), and that judge AA has an inherent bias bA​(r)b\_{A}(r) toward response rr. We approximate the score as: SA​(r)≈Q⁡(r)+bA​(r)S\_{A}(r)\\approx Q(r)+b\_{A}(r) and obtain

|  | ℙ⁡(rA≻rB∣x)=σ⁡(δ+bA),\mathbb{P}(r_{A}\succ r_{B}\mid x)=\sigma(\delta+b_{A}), |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where δ\=Q⁡(rA)−Q⁡(rB)\\delta=Q(r\_{A})-Q(r\_{B}) captures the quality gap between the two responses, and bA\=bA​(rA)−bA​(rB)b\_{A}=b\_{A}(r\_{A})-b\_{A}(r\_{B}) reflects the bias of judge AA. In the self-preference bias case, we assume that the judge exhibits bias only toward its own response, such that bA​(rB)\=0b\_{A}(r\_{B})=0 and bA\=bA​(rA)\>0b\_{A}=b\_{A}(r\_{A})>0. The expected preference probability of judge AA choosing its own response rAr\_{A} over all instructions is

|  | wA=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ+bA)].w_{A}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta+b_{A})]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

In contrast, for an unbiased gold judge, the expected preference probability of selecting rAr\_{A} is

|  | w∗=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ)].w^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta)]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

Recent work adopts metrics based on wAw\_{A} to quantify self-preference bias ([Panickssery et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib33); [Ye et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib50)). However, this approach conflates the quality of the responses with the self-preference bias of the judge model ([Chen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib6)), leading to biased estimations. Specifically, when models AA and BB correspond to a strong LLM (e.g., GPT-4o ([Hurst et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib18))) and a weaker LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct ([Grattafiori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#bib.bib13))), it becomes ambiguous whether a higher wAw\_{A} is driven by inherent differences in response quality or by the self-preference bias of the judge model AA.

To isolate the self-preference bias of model AA, we propose using the difference between the biased judge and the gold judge as a metric (referred to as the DBG score) for measuring self-preference bias:

|  | w^A=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ+bA)−σ⁡(δ)].\hat{w}_{A}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta+b_{A})-\sigma(\delta)]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

This formulation removes the confounding effect of response quality (captured by δ\\delta) and focuses explicitly on the self-preference bias term bAb\_{A}. A DBG score greater than zero indicates that the model exhibits self-preference bias, with larger values suggesting a more severe degree of bias.

When bAb\_{A} is small, a first-order Taylor approximation yields

|  | w^A≈𝔼x​[σ′​(δ)⋅bA].\hat{w}_{A}\approx\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma^{\prime}(\delta)\cdot b_{A}]. |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

Assuming a weak correlation between response quality gaps and self-preference bias of AA, we have

|  | w^A≈𝔼x​[σ′​(δ)]⋅𝔼x​[bA],\hat{w}_{A}\approx\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma^{\prime}(\delta)]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{x}[b_{A}], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

suggesting that w^A\\hat{w}\_{A} serves as a linearly scaled estimator of the true bias. Thus, it offers a more accurate and disentangled measure of self-preference than wAw\_{A}.

In practice, we aggregate the judgment results from three strong LLMs to construct the unbiased gold judgment:

|  | w^∗=𝔼x,k​[σ⁡(δ+bk)],\hat{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x,k}[\sigma(\delta+b_{k})], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where bkb\_{k} denotes the bias of model kk toward rAr\_{A}. Using the Taylor expansion, we obtain:

|  | w^∗=𝔼x​[σ⁡(δ)]+𝔼x,k​[Δ],\hat{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma(\delta)]+\mathbb{E}_{x,k}[\Delta], |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

where Δ\\Delta represents the remainder term. If the bias of each individual model is relatively small or fluctuates around zero, then Δ≈0\\Delta\\approx 0. This indicates that aggregation helps mitigate the bias of any single model and enhances the stability of the evaluation. Additionally, to further validate the reliability of the gold judgments, we conduct a human study, as detailed in Section [3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592#S3.SS3 "3.3 Alignment Between Gold Judgments and Human Annotations ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments").

## 8 Limitations

##### Single-Turn Interaction.

Our simulations use single-turn sessions where each instruction fully specifies a task without needing clarification.

##### Practical Constraints.

Our simulation parameters – document size (3–5k tokens), distractor context (8–12k tokens), relay length (20 interactions) – were chosen based on practical cost and context-window limits, and underestimate real-world scale.

##### Conceptual Constraints.

Our framework relies on (1) backtranslation and (2) domain-specific parsing for reference-free evaluation, which constrains scope in three ways: tasks are limited to document editing (excluding other knowledge work like communication or planning); edits must be reversible (see Appendix [B.3]); and evaluation favors structured domains where parsing is tractable. ## 8 Limitations

##### Single-Turn Interaction.

Our simulations use single-turn sessions where each instruction fully specifies a task without needing clarification. In practice, users underspecify instructions and iteratively refine intent through multi-turn conversation ([Herlihy et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib85); [Kim et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib87)), and LLM performance degrades significantly in multi-turn settings ([Laban et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib83)). Extending DELEGATE-52 to multi-turn, multi-session simulations (e.g., via instruction sharding or user simulation ([Naous et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib86))) would likely amplify degradation.

##### Practical Constraints.

Our simulation parameters – document size (3–5k tokens), distractor context (8–12k tokens), relay length (20 interactions) – were chosen based on practical cost and context-window limits, and underestimate real-world scale. Experiments show increasing these parameters worsens degradation.

##### Conceptual Constraints.

Our framework relies on (1) backtranslation and (2) domain-specific parsing for reference-free evaluation, which constrains scope in three ways: tasks are limited to document editing (excluding other knowledge work like communication or planning); edits must be reversible (see Appendix [B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#A2.SS3 "B.3 Limitations ‣ Appendix B Backtranslation Properties, Assumptions, and Limitations ‣ LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate")); and evaluation favors structured domains where parsing is tractable. We explore expansion of the framework to more open-ended generation tasks by including the Fiction domain as one of the domains in the benchmark, though it requires adapting the evaluation to leverage a specialized evaluation method ([Chakrabarty et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib84)) catered to creative writing.

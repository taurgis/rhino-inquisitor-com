### 2.2 Voice, Style, and Narrative Markers

The stakes of such mediation are methodological as well as archival, because linguistic patterning serves as evidence across multiple DH traditions. Computational stylistics operationalizes style through vocabulary distribution, syntactic structure, and punctuation usage ([Argamon, 2010]; [Herrmann et al., 2021]); authorship attribution treats such features as relatively stable individual signatures ([Stamatatos, 2009]; [Eder et al., 2016]; [Mikros and Boumparis, 2024]); work on personal narrative links linguistic form to self-presentation, memory, and narrative organization ([Pennebaker and King, 1999]; [Pennebaker et al., 2003]; [Labov and Waletzky, 1967]). The underlying claim is not that any single feature transparently reveals voice, but that patterning is analytically meaningful. This position is reinforced by recent arguments that stylometric analysis works precisely because authors write in subtly different registers ([Grieve, 2023]; [Biber and Conrad, 2019]), and that formal features are theoretically meaningful carriers of narrative structure, not mere surface patterns ([Piper et al., 2021]; [Abello et al., 2012]). Features such as contraction, pronoun use, punctuation, and lexical variation are routinely used to characterize style, register, and authorship, making their systematic alteration by AI revision methodologically consequential.

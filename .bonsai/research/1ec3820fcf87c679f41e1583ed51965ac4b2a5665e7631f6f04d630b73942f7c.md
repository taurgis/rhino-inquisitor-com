### 4.1 Heavy LLM Users Report That Their Essays Do Not Reflect Their Own Voice

Figure [4(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18161#S4.F4.sf1 "Figure 4(a) ‣ Figure 4 ‣ 4 Results ‣ How LLMs Distort Our Written Language") shows the Likert-scale self-report scores of participants evaluating the essays they produced during the user study. Both participants in the control group who did not have access to an LLM (Human User) and those who merely used the LLM as a tool to search for additional information or receive writing advice (LLM-influenced) scored similarly across all self-report metrics. However, participants who heavily relied on an LLM to help write their essay (LLM) reported feeling that their essay was significantly less creative (t⁡(69)\=2.110,p\=0.0385t(69)=2.110,p=0.0385) and less in their own voice (t⁡(69)\=5.799,p<0.001t(69)=5.799,p<0.001) using a t-test comparing to those without the LLM intervention. Paradoxically, these users reported a similar level of both satisfaction with the ultimate product as participants in the control group, as well as similar difficulty in organizing the essay and degree of writing struggle. These results shed light on the draw of using LLMs to complete writing tasks; people are satisfied with the results, even though their voice and creativity are diminished.

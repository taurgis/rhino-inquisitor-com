## 7 Related Work

Our work sits at the intersection of four research areas.

##### Evaluating AI Systems for Knowledge Work.

AI systems are increasingly adopted in knowledge work professions, with [Bick et al. (2024)] reporting that around 40% of working-age Americans used generative AI at work in late 2024, and surveys finding knowledge workers actively integrating LLMs into their workflows ([Brachman et al., 2024]; [Ulloa et al., 2025]).

The community has been hard at work building benchmarks that better capture real-world work, building industry-specific benchmarks for customer service ([Huang et al., 2024]; [Yao et al., 2024]), enterprise knowledge work ([Drouin et al., 2024]; [Xu et al., 2024]), IT operations ([Jha et al., 2025]), or spanning multiple professions ([Chen et al., 2025a]; [Patwardhan et al., 2025]; [Mazeika et al., 2025]).

Another vein of work has analyzed logs interactions, for example from users of OpenAI’s ChatGPT ([Chatterji et al., 2025]), Anthropic’s Claude ([Handa et al., 2025]), or Microsoft’s Bing Copilot ([Tomlinson et al., 2025]).

##### Benchmarking AI Systems for Document Editing.

Document editing is among the most common tasks in knowledge work ([Siu and Fok, 2025]), and one of the primary use cases of LLM-based systems ([Handa et al., 2025]; [Eloundou et al., 2023]).

For non-programmatic domains, where evaluation cannot rely on verifiable execution, more targeted benchmarks have been proposed, for instance to evaluate capabilities for news article editing ([Spangher et al., 2022]), text simplification ([Laban et al., 2023]), fiction creative writing ([Chakrabarty et al., 2024]), or instruction-following ([Raheja et al., 2023]; [Dwivedi-Yu et al., 2022]). For structured textual domains, prior work has looked at editing graphics files (SVGEditBench ([Nishina and Matsui, 2024]), SVGenius ([Chen et al., 2025b])), charts and tables (ChartEditBench ([Kapadnis et al., 2026]), WikiTableEdit ([Li et al., 2024]), ChartE3 ([Li et al., 2026])), slide decks (PPTArena ([Ofengenden et al., 2025]), DECKBench ([Jang et al., 2026])), or structured output generation across multiple formats ([Yang et al., 2025]).

This prior work typically focuses on a single domain, for which custom evaluation is curated.

##### Backtranslation.

Backtranslation (a.k.a., round-trip translation) has its roots in the neural machine translation (NMT) community, with early work showing that round-trip translation on monolingual corpora could be effectively leveraged to augment data and improve translation performance ([Sennrich et al., 2015]; [Lample et al., 2017]).

Backtranslation has then been successfully applied in other domains, for instance to the code domain, where it was leveraged to train unsupervised code translation models across programming languages ([Lachaux et al., 2020]; [Rozière et al., 2021]), and to jointly train code generation and summarization as dual tasks ([Wei et al., 2019]).

Some work has looked at chaining consecutive backtranslation cycles as a way to evaluate consistency or robustness of LLMs, measuring whether models preserve information through sequences of reversible transformations ([Hong et al., 2025]; [Min et al., 2023]; [Allamanis et al., 2024]; [Maveli et al., 2026]).

We extend backtranslation-as-evaluation ([Zhuo et al., 2022]; [Allamanis et al., 2024]) from single round-trips in individual domains to chained sequences across 52 diverse professions, simulating long delegated workflows where errors compound.

##### Evaluating Long, Multi-Session Interaction.

AI systems are most commonly evaluated on independent conversations (single sessions) without prior history or context. [Xu et al. (2021)] introduced the first multi-session conversation dataset, showing that models trained on single sessions fail to maintain coherent long-term dialogue, and [Jang et al. (2023)] scaled this to 1M dialogues with diverse temporal dynamics.

Since then, the community has built benchmarks to evaluate memory in LLMs across sessions.

Beyond memory, other work has studied LLM personalization across sessions: [Jiang et al. (2025)] benchmarked dynamic user profiling across 60+ sessions, [Li et al. (2025)] studied implicit preference reasoning, and [Mehri et al. (2026)] evaluated how agents learn collaborative preferences over time. Recent work has also extended multi-session evaluation to agentic systems: [Zheng et al. (2025)] benchmarked lifelong learning in LLM agents, [He et al. (2026)] tested memory in interdependent multi-session tasks, and [Du et al. (2025)] introduced the first multi-session task-oriented dialogue benchmark.

Prior work frames multi-session interaction as fundamentally a memory problem: can the system remember, retrieve, or adapt based on past interactions?

## 7 Related Work

Our work sits at the intersection of four research areas.

##### Evaluating AI Systems for Knowledge Work.

AI systems are increasingly adopted in knowledge work professions, with [Bick et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib14) reporting that around 40% of working-age Americans used generative AI at work in late 2024, and surveys finding knowledge workers actively integrating LLMs into their workflows ([Brachman et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib81); [Ulloa et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib89)). Yet, existing evaluation benchmarks have been shown to be misaligned with real-world use ([Wang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib2)).

The community has been hard at work building benchmarks that better capture real-world work, building industry-specific benchmarks for customer service ([Huang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib6); [Yao et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib18)), enterprise knowledge work ([Drouin et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib15); [Xu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib16)), IT operations ([Jha et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib10)), or spanning multiple professions ([Chen et al., 2025a](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib7); [Patwardhan et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib5); [Mazeika et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib4)). However, such benchmarks require costly expert annotation, often limiting the scope of the benchmark.

Another vein of work has analyzed logs interactions, for example from users of OpenAI’s ChatGPT ([Chatterji et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib13)), Anthropic’s Claude ([Handa et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib3)), or Microsoft’s Bing Copilot ([Tomlinson et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib1)). Researchers then can connect interactions with work task taxonomies such as O\*NET ([Peterson et al., 2001](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib19)), gaining perspective on current work practices. This research however requires careful handling of privacy-sensitive data, and is limited to the few organizations that have access to interaction logs at scale.

##### Benchmarking AI Systems for Document Editing.

Document editing is among the most common tasks in knowledge work ([Siu and Fok, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib8)), and one of the primary use cases of LLM-based systems ([Handa et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib3); [Eloundou et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib11)). This has spurred active research communities that study AI system capabilities to edit documents.

For non-programmatic domains, where evaluation cannot rely on verifiable execution, more targeted benchmarks have been proposed, for instance to evaluate capabilities for news article editing ([Spangher et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib33)), text simplification ([Laban et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib34)), fiction creative writing ([Chakrabarty et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib73)), or instruction-following ([Raheja et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib26); [Dwivedi-Yu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib25)). For structured textual domains, prior work has looked at editing graphics files (SVGEditBench ([Nishina and Matsui, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib20)), SVGenius ([Chen et al., 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib27))), charts and tables (ChartEditBench ([Kapadnis et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib23)), WikiTableEdit ([Li et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib28)), ChartE3 ([Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib32))), slide decks (PPTArena ([Ofengenden et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib21)), DECKBench ([Jang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib22))), or structured output generation across multiple formats ([Yang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib82)).

This prior work typically focuses on a single domain, for which custom evaluation is curated. With DELEGATE-52, we take a more generalizable approach that enables us to extend our methodology to 52 domains: we develop programmatic domain-specific parsers for each domain, and leverage a backtranslation-based evaluation that circumvents the need for references.

##### Backtranslation.

Backtranslation (a.k.a., round-trip translation) has its roots in the neural machine translation (NMT) community, with early work showing that round-trip translation on monolingual corpora could be effectively leveraged to augment data and improve translation performance ([Sennrich et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib36); [Lample et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib47)). Beyond data augmentation, backtranslation has been used as a direct training signal through dual learning, where forward and backward models are jointly optimized via round-trip consistency ([He et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib37); [Hoang et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib39)), and as a reference-free evaluation method, where round-trip fidelity serves as a proxy for translation quality ([Somers, 2005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib44); [Zhuo et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib41)).

Backtranslation has then been successfully applied in other domains, for instance to the code domain, where it was leveraged to train unsupervised code translation models across programming languages ([Lachaux et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib46); [Rozière et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib48)), and to jointly train code generation and summarization as dual tasks ([Wei et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib45)). More recently, back-translation has been applied to instruction following to improve LLM alignment ([Li et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib38); [Nguyen et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib40)).

Some work has looked at chaining consecutive backtranslation cycles as a way to evaluate consistency or robustness of LLMs, measuring whether models preserve information through sequences of reversible transformations ([Hong et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib29); [Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib42); [Allamanis et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib35); [Maveli et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib43)).

We extend backtranslation-as-evaluation ([Zhuo et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib41); [Allamanis et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib35)) from single round-trips in individual domains to chained sequences across 52 diverse professions, simulating long delegated workflows where errors compound. This reduces evaluation to measuring semantic equivalence with the original document, allowing us to scale evaluation across domains without requiring annotation.

##### Evaluating Long, Multi-Session Interaction.

AI systems are most commonly evaluated on independent conversations (single sessions) without prior history or context. [Xu et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib58) introduced the first multi-session conversation dataset, showing that models trained on single sessions fail to maintain coherent long-term dialogue, and [Jang et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib59) scaled this to 1M dialogues with diverse temporal dynamics.

Since then, the community has built benchmarks to evaluate memory in LLMs across sessions. [Maharana et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib54) evaluated very long-term conversational memory, and [Wu et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib50) proposed LongMemEval, benchmarking core memory abilities (retention, retrieval, synthesis) in chat assistants, extended by more recent benchmarks such as EverMemBench ([Hu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib53)) and LifeBench ([Cheng et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib55)) testing memory across hundreds of interactions and diverse information sources.

Beyond memory, other work has studied LLM personalization across sessions: [Jiang et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib57) benchmarked dynamic user profiling across 60+ sessions, [Li et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib60) studied implicit preference reasoning, and [Mehri et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib56) evaluated how agents learn collaborative preferences over time. Recent work has also extended multi-session evaluation to agentic systems: [Zheng et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib51) benchmarked lifelong learning in LLM agents, [He et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib52) tested memory in interdependent multi-session tasks, and [Du et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597#bib.bib49) introduced the first multi-session task-oriented dialogue benchmark.

Prior work frames multi-session interaction as fundamentally a memory problem: can the system remember, retrieve, or adapt based on past interactions? With DELEGATE-52, we study an orthogonal and understudied failure mode: whether repeated LLM interaction degrades the artifacts being worked on. We study how model errors in early sessions compound and affect long-horizon performance.

## Detailed

## **Try It**

I have tried to outline some of my reasoning on why using the Five Whys approach is suboptimal, and I’ve given an alternative. I’ll do one better and link you to the tutorials that I gave in New York in October, which I think digs deeper into these concepts. This is in four parts, 45 minutes each.

[**Part I – Introduction and the scientific basis for post-hoc restrospective pitfalls and learning**](http://player.oreilly.com/videos/9781491900390?toc_id=191352&intcmp=il-velocity-free-article-vleu14_allspaw_tutorial_p1 "PostMortem Debriefings Part I")

**[Part II – The language of debriefings, causality, case studies, teams coping with complexity](http://player.oreilly.com/videos/9781491900390?toc_id=191353&intcmp=il-velocity-free-article-vleu14_allspaw_tutorial_p2 "New View Debriefings Part II")  
**

**[Part III – Dynamic fault management, debriefing prompts, gathering and contextualizing data, constructing causes](http://player.oreilly.com/videos/9781491900390?toc_id=191354&intcmp=il-velocity-free-article-vleu14_allspaw_tutorial_p3 "New View Debriefings Part III")**

**[Part IV – Taylorism, normal work, ‘root cause’ of software bugs in cars, Q&A](http://player.oreilly.com/videos/9781491900390?toc_id=191355&intcmp=il-velocity-free-article-vleu14_allspaw_tutorial_p4 "New View Debriefings Part IV")  
**

My request is that the next time that you would do a Five Whys, that you instead ask “how?” or the variations of the questions I posted above. If you think you get _more_ operational data from a Five Whys and are happy with it, rock on.

If you’re more interested in this alternative and the fundamentals behind it, then there are a number of sources you can look to. You could do a lot worse than starting with Sidney Dekker’s _[Field Guide To Understanding Human Error](https://bookshop.org/p/books/the-field-guide-to-understanding-human-error-sidney-dekker/3a4209dfc8b3a721 "Field Guide To Understanding Human Error")._

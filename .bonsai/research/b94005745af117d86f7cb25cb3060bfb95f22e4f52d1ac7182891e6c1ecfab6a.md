## Detailed

### B.3 Characteristic Phrases

We provide additional results for characteristic phrases as presented in Section [3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150#S3.SS1 "3.1 Words and Letters ‣ 3 Concrete Idiosyncrasies in LLMs ‣ Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models"). We follow the same methodology in Figure [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150#S3.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ 3.1 Words and Letters ‣ 3 Concrete Idiosyncrasies in LLMs ‣ Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models") to extract characteristic phrases of instruct and base LLMs. Specifically, we train a four-way logistic regression classifier on the TF-IDF features of their responses and use the coefficients to select important phrases of each model.

As shown in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150#footnote2 "footnote 2 ‣ Figure 14 ‣ B.3 Characteristic Phrases ‣ Appendix B Additional Results ‣ Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models"), each instruct LLM contains quite distinct characteristic phrases. For example, Llama frequently employs terms “including” and “such as” to introduce specific examples in the output, whereas Gemma tends to engage with users using phrases “let me” and “know if”. In contrast, the extracted phrases from base LLMs are less distinctive, primarily consisting of common words such as “the”, “to”, and “you”.

Figure [15](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150#A2.F15 "Figure 15 ‣ B.3 Characteristic Phrases ‣ Appendix B Additional Results ‣ Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models") illustrates the distribution of first word choices in instruct and base LLMs. Similar to chat APIs (Figure [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150#S3.F7 "Figure 7 ‣ 3.1 Words and Letters ‣ 3 Concrete Idiosyncrasies in LLMs ‣ Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models")), instruct LLMs display varied distributions. However, base LLMs exhibit substantial overlap in their most frequent first words, _e.g_., “the”, “and”, “of”, “to”, and “in”.
